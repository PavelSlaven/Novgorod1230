Scope: research only. No historical-use conclusion, line materialization,
numeric capacity, load calculation, knot recipe, or outcome is proposed.

can reduce available strength. It amends PE-03's single fibre-rope candidate;
it is not a second rope claim.

## Independently read authoritative sources

1. [U.S. Army TM 3-34.86 / Marine Corps MCRP
   3-17.7J](https://rdl.train.army.mil/catalog-ws/view/100.ATSC/1676C9DD-7ADF-4E41-9130-6BCEE7BBC5A9-1343151324082/tm3_34x86.pdf),
   §§1-12–1-15, “Fiber Rope / Strength.”  It identifies Manila and sisal as
   fibre ropes and says strength is reduced after use and weather exposure; it
   separately names shock loading, knots and sharp bends as stresses that can
   reduce rope strength. The manual’s tables and safety factors are excluded.
2. [Washington Administrative Code 296-24-29403, “Hemp
   Rope”](https://app.leg.wa.gov/wac/default.aspx?cite=296-24-29403),
   subsections (1), (4)–(7). It calls for source-labelled “Manila hemp rope”
   at sharp/abrasive contact to be protected, knot ends to be secured against
   untying, and badly frayed/rotted/defective rope to be rejected. This is a
   modern occupational rule, not a universal physical threshold.

## Botanical/material limit

The WAC’s phrase “Manila hemp” is a source label for a regulated rope class; it
does **not** independently identify that class as *Cannabis sativa* hemp.
The Army source establishes Manila/sisal natural-fibre rope, not a medieval
Novgorod line. Therefore neither source transfers an exact material property to
the pack’s historical `hemp-fibre` concept.

## PE-03 amendment rationale

**RU:** У волокнистой верёвки истирание, изгибы и узлы могут уменьшать
доступную прочность.

**EN:** In fibre rope, abrasion, bends, and knots can reduce available strength.

**Anchor:** Army TM 3-34.86/MCRP 3-17.7J §§1-12–1-15. WAC 296-24-29403 is
corroborative for abrasion, fraying and knot-security handling, not a numerical
or historical source.

**Limits:** This is a medium-confidence inference across modern synthetic and
natural-fibre manuals. No exact capacity, proportion of loss, safe load, knot
identity, secure/unsafe conclusion, break, present line, repair success, or
actor knowledge follows. It does not make modern Manila/sisal evidence into an
exact claim about historical Cannabis-hemp construction or practice. Combine
with existing friction only to rule out unconditional hold; the mechanics owner
still decides actual tension, contact, geometry and result.

## Unseen probes

- A frayed manila hauling line over a rough edge: the candidate can make
  condition relevant; it does not decide breakage or a safe pull.
- A player knots an unspecified historical hemp cord: current friction says it
  is not unconditionally secure. The candidate cannot supply its capacity or
  transfer modern Manila/sisal behaviour to it.
- A clean natural-fibre line under a sudden pull: prior load/shock is a
  condition question, not a fixed strength number or automatic failure.
