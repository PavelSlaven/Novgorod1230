# Fuel and lighting forms — charcoal research packet

Candidates await independent verification; no production facts are proposed.
They describe historical process compatibility only, never a present fuel
stock, kiln, skill, ignition, fire, or successful metallurgical outcome.

## Source K — Kolchin, ferrous metallurgy of Novgorod

[B. A. Kolchin, *Ferrous metallurgy and metalworking in Ancient Rus*, MIA 32
(1953)](https://archaeolog.ru/media/series/mia/MIA_32.pdf), scanned local copy
`.tmp-pr92-sources/mia-32.pdf`, inspected 2026-09-03. Chapter V, printed p. 40
(*Топливо*) says abundant forest and the simple technique of charcoal-burning
provided Ancient Rus metallurgy with fuel; it reconstructs pit charcoal-making
as the likely method. The scan's p. 168 also captions a figure *Светцы и
подсвечники*, but no dated Novgorod provenance or operational passage was
located, so it yields **no lighting candidate**.

## Scope and overlap

K01–K07 are wider Ancient-Rus compatibility, with a historical process
reconstructed by Kolchin rather than an exact 1230 Novgorod site inventory.
They should use `domain_internal_only`, inferred/medium for K02–K07, and avoid
numeric burn durations. Existing metalworking context already states that a
specific hearth, fuel and process require scene state; these candidates must
not weaken that boundary.

The only actual medieval-lighting lead found remains a 2009 media report about
absence of splinter residues in XI–early-XIII Troitsky layers and its mention
of candles/oil lamps. Its underlying excavation publication was not located;
do not promote it. Do not infer a lamp, candle, tallow, beeswax, splinter or
successful lighting from this packet.
