# Terrain and mineral-resource context — bounded follow-up

**Status:** research candidates only. Nothing here self-approves production,
materializes an outcrop, quarry, pit, stock, route, worker, church, or grants
permission to collect or remove material.

## Gap checked

The archived coverage matrix marks both `wk:env:terrain` and
`wk:env:raw-sources` **P1 / partial**. Existing terrain production supplies
only a modern regional Quaternary parent-material envelope; existing universal
claims cover physical clay and stone response. Neither establishes an
extractable medieval deposit. This pass therefore sought dated evidence of
mineral-material procurement and transformation, rather than treating a modern
map as proof of a 1230 source.

## Source independently read

V. A. Bulkin and I. V. Antipov, [«Об особенностях организации строительства и
строительного производства в Новгородской земле в домонгольское время»](https://doi.org/10.18688/aa177-4-40),
*Актуальные проблемы теории и истории искусства* 7 (2017), pp. 410–418,
open-access PDF at the [journal record](https://publ.actual-art.org/aptha/ru/article/view/10572).
The full nine-page PDF was opened in the browser; the article’s abstract and
concluding discussion (p. 415 / PDF p. 6) were read. The authors study
pre-Mongol Novgorod Land **monumental stone-church construction**, so every
candidate below is specialist/project context, not ordinary domestic building.

## Result and boundary of this evidence

This source supplies **five** usable project-level historical premises. Its
strongest resource finding is the opposite of a free local-source inference:
limestone and shell rock are described as quarry-procured for specialist
projects. For ordinary resource materialization, the existing qualified
regional material-class envelope and universal physical extraction constraints
remain the factual substrate; the state/profile owner, not world knowledge,
must establish a particular source, access, quantity and location. I also searched
the accessible full record for Novgorod pottery/resource work; an accessible
Plokhov 2021 record identifies *Было ли в средневековом Новгороде гончарное
производство?* (pp. 218–248) but placed the full PDF behind Academia access,
so it is not used as evidence here.

This pass does not provide a direct medieval attestation of a clay pit, bank
collection of sand/gravel/pebbles, or a local limestone exposure. Those would
be useful only for a narrower historical technology or source-class envelope;
they are not prerequisites for a profile-owned ordinary scene source. The
modern Quaternary terrain envelope remains qualified geological compatibility,
not a location or inventory assertion.
