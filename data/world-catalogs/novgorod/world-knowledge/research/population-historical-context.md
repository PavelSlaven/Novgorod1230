# Novgorod c.1230: material and occupation context for water-edge ordinary locations

**Status:** research candidates only; no `review_status` decision, runtime profile, item list, recipe, or presence grant is made here. Intended contexts: an already-authorized riverbank, fishing/work camp, drying/work shelter, or storehouse in Novgorod land around 1230.

## Evidence opened

1. **E1 — E. A. Rybina, “Промыслы в средневековом Новгороде (по археологическим материалам)”**, *Исторические исследования* 3 (2015), pp. 219–235, DOI `10.24412/FdmM6oz9S8g`, [full text](https://cyberleninka.ru/article/n/promysly-v-srednevekovom-novgorode-po-arheologicheskim-materialam). Opened source. Its abstract and pp. 219–223 state that archaeological finds document fishing/hunting equipment; p. 220 treats fishing as a major occupation in Novgorod land, notes fish remains and gear, and pp. 221–223 describe wood/bark, stone and iron components and net/float forms. It is archaeological scholarship, broad medieval chronology unless a dated item is stated.
2. **E2 — Birch-bark letter NGB 73**, [database record](https://gramoty.ru/birchbark/document/show/novgorod/73/). Opened primary record: Novgorod, Nerev excavation (estate B), stratigraphic date 1220–1240 / 1220s–1230s, whole document, business/debt record. It directly anchors birch bark as a written carrier in the target period, not fishing, storage, or clothing.
3. **E3 — C. Orton, “Medieval Novgorod,”** *Archaeology International* (UCL Press), [PDF](https://journals.uclpress.co.uk/ai/article/575/galley/12753/view/). Opened academic synthesis/PDF. Use only for broad waterlogged urban archaeological context and organic-material visibility; it is not a 1230 site-phase inventory.
4. **E4 — E. A. Rybina, “Мир вещей средневекового Новгорода (по археологическим находкам)”**, *Вестник Новгородского государственного университета* (2006), [full text](https://cyberleninka.ru/article/n/mir-veschey-srednevekovogo-novgoroda-po-arheologicheskim-nahodkam). Opened source. Its material-culture overview says the Novgorod excavation collection contains iron, non-ferrous metals, bone, stone, clay, glass, amber, leather, wood and birch bark; names household vessels, craft tools, transport, and residential/economic/production structures. The leather section identifies shoes, sheaths/cases, purses, mittens, belts and straps; it dates the numerous knife cases mainly to the 13th–14th centuries and reports shoe production/repair workshops through products, offcuts, patterns and wooden lasts. Broad medieval assemblage, not a single 1230 yard inventory.
5. **E5 — Novgorod State Museum-Reserve, “Обувь древнего Новгорода X–XV веков”** (2021 exhibition record), [museum record](https://novgorodmuseum.ru/o-muzee-zapovednike/novosti/obuv-drevnego-novgoroda-x-xv-vekov). Opened source. It reports archaeological footwear/types, shoemaking tools and costume-related items; explicitly says shoemaking workshops appeared on urban estates in the 12th–13th centuries as the craft separated from tanning, and that tanned cattle/small-ruminant hides were used. This is an authoritative museum synthesis, broad X–XV chronology except its stated 12th–13th workshop horizon.

## Production-facing limits

- **Availability is not presence.** Every positive candidate is historical compatibility only. Existing spatial, source, ownership, container, visibility, time and process owners alone determine concrete presence and change.
- **Process is not recipe.** PHC-04 and PHC-13 describe necessary classes of conditions, not sufficient steps, fixed outputs, exact durations or quality values.
- **Universal and historical layers remain separate.** Heat, buoyancy, water response, cutting and spoilage require universal-science primitives; this shard supplies only regional/historical availability evidence.
- **No occupation stereotyping.** Fishing evidence does not determine an NPC’s identity, legal status, competence, gender, age, clothing, wealth, motive, schedule or relationship.
- **Anachronism guard.** Nothing here supports synthetic net fibre, rubber clothing, industrial hooks, modern preservatives, engines, factory containers, modern food-safety thresholds, or a modern harbour infrastructure.
