# B11: жвачные, бобовые корма и плесневый корм

## Scope

Кандидат закрывает только две недостающие причинные связи: риск пенистого вздутия у жвачного после бобового корма и пределы вывода из видимой плесени в корме. Это современная ветеринарная/кормовая наука, не историческое знание персонажа XIII века, не диагноз конкретного животного и не лечебная инструкция.

## Sources read

- [Merck Veterinary Manual — Bloat in Ruminants](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/digestive-system/diseases-of-the-ruminant-forestomach/bloat-in-ruminants), sections “Etiology and Pathogenesis” and “Clinical Findings”. It describes primary/frothy bloat as normal fermentation gas trapped in stable foam, identifies legume-dominant pasture including clover as a common context, and describes marked distension and dyspnea in severe progression. It also makes clear that bloat has distinct causes; no diagnosis follows from one scene description.
- [University of Minnesota Extension — Mycotoxins harm swine health and reproductive performance](https://extension.umn.edu/agriculture/animals-and-livestock/swine/mycotoxins-harm-swine-health-and-reproductive-performance), sections “What are mycotoxins?” and “Testing for mycotoxins in grains or feeds”. It states that some grain molds produce mycotoxins harmful to animals, that mycotoxins are not present in all moldy grain, and that effects vary with toxin level and animal factors; it directs laboratory analysis for toxin presence and level.

## Candidate bounds

- No drench, trocar, surgery, drug, dose, feed-ration or historical treatment claim.
- Clover is a risk context, not proof of cause; ruminant species, amount eaten, duration, rumen state and outcome remain unestablished.
- Visible mold is neither proof nor measurement of a mycotoxin. “Clean” or alternative feed must exist and be assessed separately; replacing suspect feed is not a magic safety guarantee.
- Livestock and poultry sensitivity cannot be treated as one identical response.
