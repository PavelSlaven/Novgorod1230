# Macro batch 17 gap closure — source rationale v1

## Scope

This candidate closes three reusable, practical premises revealed by static
batch 17.  It does not identify a particular haystack, plant, insect,
substance, dye recipe, person, diagnosis, availability of help, or outcome.

## Damp stored hay

North Dakota State University Extension describes excessive moisture as the
main cause of hay fires: high-moisture stored hay can heat, develop a musty or
caramel smell, and in severe cases smoulder or ignite.  Its immediate safety
advice is deliberately reduced here to the transferable minimum: do not pull
apart a suspected hot or smoking mass, keep people and animals clear, and seek
available fire assistance.  No moisture, temperature, storage method, timing,
or fire outcome is retained.

## Sticky plant coatings

University of Maryland Extension identifies honeydew as a sugary waste product
of many sap-sucking insects.  Cornell's poisonous-plant reference separately
shows that fresh sap from some plants can irritate skin.  Together these facts
support only a cautious field inference: a sticky coating may have more than
one cause, including honeydew, and its appearance alone cannot identify the
cause or establish safety.  They do not identify an insect, plant, coating,
injury, or diagnosis in a scene.

## Unknown dye-bath contact

ATSDR's guidance for an unidentified chemical treats the material's identity
and effects as unknown, calls for removing contaminated clothing and flushing
exposed or irritated skin with plain water, and directs further assessment for
serious or persistent concerns.  The candidate transfers only that generic
first-response order to an already established unknown dye or mordant bath;
it does not name the bath composition, toxicity, dose, diagnosis, treatment,
or outcome.
