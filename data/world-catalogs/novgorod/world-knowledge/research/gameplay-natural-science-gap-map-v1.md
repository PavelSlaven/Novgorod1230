It compares the current 120-family cartography and its nine explicit partial families with ordinary unseen actions; it does not infer gaps from claim count. ### NS-01 — combustion products in enclosed air

- **Potential domains/consumer:** `chemistry_process` / `physics_material_science`; potential F1 hearth, S1 interior and A1 open-action grounding.
- **Candidate mechanism:** `fuel combustion can produce carbon monoxide; in an enclosed indoor space carbon monoxide can accumulate and inhalation can cause illness or death.`
- **RU:** «При горении топлива может образовываться угарный газ; в закрытом помещении он может накапливаться, а его вдыхание может вызвать тяжёлое отравление.»
- **EN:** “Fuel combustion can produce carbon monoxide; it can build up in an enclosed indoor space, and inhalation can cause severe poisoning.”
- **Evidence:** CDC, *Carbon Monoxide Poisoning Basics*, lines 25, 45 and 77–81: CO is odorless/colorless, fuel-burning fumes can build up indoors, and burning charcoal indoors produces CO. URL: <https://www.cdc.gov/carbon-monoxide/about/index.html>.
- **Limits:** No present fire, fuel, enclosure, ventilation state, concentration, exposure duration, symptom, diagnosis, death, or outcome. No player safety instruction.

### NS-02 — cold-water immersion cooling

- **Potential domains/consumer:** `biology_physiology`; potential body/temperature owner, river crossing or rescue action grounding.
- **Candidate mechanism:** `cold-water immersion can produce immersion hypothermia more quickly than standard hypothermia.`
- **RU:** «Погружение в холодную воду может привести к переохлаждению от погружения быстрее, чем обычное переохлаждение.»
- **EN:** “Cold-water immersion can produce immersion hypothermia more quickly than standard hypothermia.”
- **Evidence:** CDC/NIOSH, *Cold-related Illnesses in Workers*, section “Immersion hypothermia from cold water,” lines 67–73. URL: <https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/cold-stress/about/related-illness.html>.
- **Limits:** No water temperature, immersion duration, clothing state, actor temperature, impairment stage, survival time, recovery, injury, treatment, or forced action. Exact body effects stay body/time owners.

### NS-03 — boiling: germs versus dissolved chemicals

- **Potential domains/consumer:** `chemistry_process` / `biology_physiology`; potential water/food handling and A1 process grounding.
- **Candidate mechanism:** `boiling or heating water can kill disease-causing germs, but it does not make water containing harmful chemicals drinkable.`
- **RU:** «Кипячение или нагрев воды может уничтожать болезнетворные микроорганизмы, но не делает пригодной для питья воду с вредными химическими примесями.»
- **EN:** “Boiling or heating water can kill disease-causing germs, but it does not make water containing harmful chemicals drinkable.”
- **Evidence:** CDC, *Household Water Treatment*, lines 46–59: boiling/heating kills viruses, bacteria and parasites; fuel, toxic chemicals or radioactive material are not made drinkable by treatment. URL: <https://www.cdc.gov/global-water-sanitation-hygiene/about/about-household-water-treatment.html>.
- **Limits:** No local water quality, germs, chemicals, pot, fuel, boil event, treatment success, drinking permission, dose, illness, or medical advice. This does not turn unclear water into contaminated or potable water.
