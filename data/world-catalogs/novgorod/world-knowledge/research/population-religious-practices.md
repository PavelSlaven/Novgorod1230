# Christian institutional and practice substrate — research candidates

**Status:** research only; no production or self-approval. This pass seeks a
general Old Rus institutional substrate, not evidence that a particular NPC
believes, knows doctrine, is clergy, attends a rite, owns a church, or carries
out a practice in a 1230 scene.

## Existing-corpus boundary

Already compiled Novgorod letter facts about priest address, Christian text,
calendar text, commemorations, and church-payment documents were read and not
repeated. In particular this pass does not reuse letters 1004, 616, 76, 739,
907 or 545. A calendar record or a priest’s surviving address is not a general
religious-role substrate.

3. Stolypin Museum of Russian Reforms, [*Устав князя Владимира Святославича о
   десятинах, судах и людях церковных*](http://xn--e1aaejmenocxq.xn--p1ai/node/13619)
   and [*Устав князя Ярослава Владимировича о церковных
   судах*](http://xn--e1aaejmenocxq.xn--p1ai/node/13620), both complete public
   HTML pages independently read. The museum describes the oldest Vladimir
   Olenin redaction as one of two XII–XIII redactions, with the textual basis
   of surviving copies probably compiled in the mid/second half of the XII
   century; it describes the oldest Yaroslav Extended redaction as compiled in
   the XII–first quarter of the XIII century. Both pages explicitly stress
   editorial layering and debate, and reproduce the text from *Российское
   законодательство X–XX веков*, vol. 1 (1984), pp. 139–140.

**Dating limit.** The museum’s date statements permit a **broad Old Rus
XII–XIII legal-tradition compatibility** envelope, but not an unqualified local
Novgorod 1230 statute: both textual histories have layering and the surviving
manuscript basis is later. Treat every candidate as inferred/medium for
1100–1300 compatibility, never as actual enforcement or present social state.

## Non-candidates and guardrails

- Existing Novgorod calendar/commemoration and Christian-text records remain
  their own evidence; they are not duplicated here.
- No candidate says that worship, burial, fasts, prayer, baptism, marriage,
  charity or hospital care occurred in a particular scene.
- Do not derive a concrete priest, monastery, workshop-religion site, church,
  funeral, calendar date, sacred object, social rank, legal penalty, or actor
  knowledge from these broad documentary categories.

## Suggested verification focus

An independent verifier should independently confirm the museum’s dating and
editorial boundary. If that XII–XIII envelope is not accepted, reject use for
c.1230 rather than treating an attributed statute as contemporaneous. If
accepted, promote at most RP-01–RP-04 in their narrow wording; RP-05 is
potentially redundant with RP-02 and should be omitted unless a
social-jurisdiction query demonstrably needs it.

## Addendum: personal religious sign and burial assemblage

### Source independently opened

State Historical Museum, [*Wax candles and a next-to-skin cross – a complex
of items from a woman's burial*](https://nav.shm.ru/en/exhibits/1438/),
complete public exhibit page, opened 2026-09-04.  It identifies mound C-301,
Gnezdovo (Smolensk Oblast), as a woman's burial of the second half of the X
century.  Its descriptive text calls the object a “cross worn next to the
skin”; the cross has a fixed suspension loop.  The interpretive paragraph
calls this a rare Christian burial and explicitly says that its ritual
combined new Christian symbols with older pagan traditions.  It also says
such finds are associated in historiography with elite druzhina burials.

| ID | Relation (RU / EN) | Exact textual basis | Scope and hard limits |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| RPO-01 | **Нательный крест → мог носиться → непосредственно на теле. / A next-to-skin cross → could be worn → directly on the body.** | The catalogue’s object field says “Size of the cross worn next to the skin”; the description says the thin silver cross has a fixed suspension loop. | Direct/high for this Gnezdovo burial object, second half X c.; only earlier-Old-Rus **inferred/low–medium** compatibility for Novgorod Land 1100–1300. It does not establish a cross, wearer, metal, wealth, gender, belief, conversion, church attendance, or current personal possession in a scene. |
| RPO-02 | **Погребальный ритуал с христианскими символами → мог сочетаться → с более ранними нехристианскими традициями. / A burial rite with Christian symbols → could combine with → older non-Christian traditions.** | The exhibit’s synthesis: “the ritual had both new cultural symbols (Christian) and old pagan traditions.” | Direct/high only for the exhibit’s late-X/early-XI comparative burial interpretation; any 1100–1300 Novgorod use is **inferred/low** and must remain an archaeological-cultural caveat, not an ordinary ritual rule. It proves no person's faith, funeral, prayer, clergy, rite sequence, grave goods, burial right, or current religious practice. |

**Disposition.** RPO-01 is the only potentially useful material/practice
premise, and then only with its earlier, elite and regional-transfer limits.
RPO-02 should normally remain research context rather than production
retrieval: it prevents treating Christian symbols as proof of a uniform rite.
