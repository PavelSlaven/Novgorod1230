# Food preparation and provisioning practices — research candidates

**Status:** research only.  These are not self-approved and do not change
production or the coverage matrix.  They add historical practice candidates
around grain preparation and provisioning; they do not materialize food,
fuel, water, a hearth, storage, a person, a crop, an animal, a recipe, or an
edible/safe outcome.

## Existing approved boundary checked first

The 442 pack already contains universal dough/baking and fermentation
mechanisms, historical oven compatibility, rye/barley and malt commodity
contexts, fish as dried/lightly salted/salted forms, and underfloor storage.
Those are deliberately not repeated.  In particular, a malt payment record is
not brewing evidence, and the already approved fish-preservation relation does
not prove a salt source, drying building, safe batch, or exact 1230 process.

## Explicit holds and exclusions

- The Borkova source records hop seeds, but explicitly gives several possible
  uses (brewing, bread flavour/preservation, other products, medicinal
  mixtures).  **Hop remains → brewing** is therefore **not a candidate**.
- The Borkova authors say grain outer coverings may be whole-grain-product or
  bran residues, and cannot decide between those alternatives.  No forced
  bran-processing relation is proposed.
- Neither source supplies a dated Novgorod malt-to-brew chain, starter,
  vessel, boiling/mashing sequence, fermentation batch, or preservation safety
  result.  Existing universal fermentation facts remain mechanism only.
- No fact above identifies a menu, compulsory diet, household class, person,
  season, field, food stock, or current cooking/storage scene.

## Result

Six candidates supply missing historically bounded grain-use, preparation and
provisioning relations.  They do not close the separate historical brewing or
safe-preservation gap, and no production promotion is implied.
