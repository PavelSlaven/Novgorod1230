# Historical household and religious-practice residuals

## Scope and method

This bounded pass checks the practical care/dependency and religious-practice/calendar residuals without asserting a live household, a local custom, or a universal obligation. It uses the existing corpus where its already-approved premises compose the gameplay need, and adds one non-duplicative textual calendar relation.

## Existing care/dependency support — no new claim

`claim:rp-minor-children-conditional-care`, `claim:rp-caretaker-principal-goods`, and `claim:rp-mother-household-care-succession` already preserve the bounded Extended *Russkaya Pravda* §93/§96 relations: in stated succession circumstances, a nearest relative receives children, house and goods; principal goods are returned; and a maternal share can attach to the household carer. `claim:social-kin-letter-reports-protection-request` and `claim:social-kin-request-reports-contingent-return` add early-thirteenth-century Novgorod documentary examples of kin-directed protection and a contingent request.

Together these support conditional dependency, provision, custody of goods, and kin negotiation. They do not create a present dependant, caregiver, household duty, motive, gender role, local custom, procedure, entitlement, or enforceable Novgorod-1230 law. A further general claim would only restate those bounded premises.

## New calendar relation

The [Church monthbook of the thirteenth-century *Obikhod*](https://sedmitza.ru/lib/text/443910/) is published by the Church Scientific Centre “Orthodox Encyclopedia”, citing Makary (Bulgakov), *History of the Russian Church*, book 2, p. 585. Its title identifies the source as a thirteenth-century monthbook. In its September section, numbered days are paired with named commemorations and feasts; for example, 8 September is the Nativity of the Mother of God and 14 September the Exaltation of the Cross.

The candidate therefore adds only `claim:historical-xiii-century-church-monthbook-associates-days-with-commemorations`: a thirteenth-century textual day-to-commemoration relation. It does not make the monthbook a Novgorod-1230 local calendar, establish observance or attendance, or determine a scene date. It complements rather than replaces `claim:social-church-calendar-text-relates-feast-list`, whose evidence is an eleventh-century Novgorod list without this day mapping.
