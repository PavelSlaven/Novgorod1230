# Population causal materials — bounded science notes

Status: research input, not authoritative world fact.
Scope: universal material relations usable only after code establishes actual material, condition, amount, geometry, tools, heat, time and access. None of these relations proves a thing exists, is available, succeeds, or was historically used at a particular Novgorod site in 1230.

## Atomic claim candidates

1. `wood -- exchanges bound water with surrounding air --> moisture content changes toward environmental equilibrium.`
   - Kind: universal material relation. Source: S1, “Wood-moisture relations”.
   - Limit: species, grain, initial moisture, temperature, airflow and exposure affect rate and amount; does not identify wood species or current moisture.

2. `wood below fibre-saturation point -- loses bound water --> shrinks, chiefly across rather than along grain.`
   - Kind: universal material relation. Source: S1, “Dimensional changes”.
   - Limit: magnitude is species- and grain-dependent; no deterministic crack or final dimension follows.

3. `wet wood -- provides conditions for fungal decay only when susceptible wood, suitable temperature, oxygen and sufficient moisture coexist --> loss of wood substance/strength over time.`
   - Kind: universal conditional relation. Source: S2, “Decay Fungi”.
   - Limit: no decay is inferred from rain or dampness alone; organism, duration and environment remain required evidence.

4. `leather exposed to very low relative humidity -- loses flexibility --> can become stiff and vulnerable to cracking when flexed.`
   - Kind: universal conditional relation. Source: S4, “Relative humidity”.
   - Limit: tanning method, prior degradation and duration matter; this does not determine an individual strap’s strength.

5. `leather kept damp in conditions favourable to mould -- supports mould growth risk --> staining and material damage may result.`
   - Kind: universal conditional relation. Source: S4, “Relative humidity”.
   - Limit: requires suitable biological conditions and time; wetness alone does not prove contamination.

6. `wood exposed to sufficient heat and oxygen -- can combust --> heat, gases and char/ash residue form.`
    - Kind: universal wood-combustion relation. Source: S5, “Combustion of Wood”.
    - Limit: applies to wood only; it makes no claim about fat, wax, resin, textile, their ignition temperature, or burn rate. Wood moisture, geometry and oxygen supply constrain onset and rate.

7. `heating wood in oxygen-limited conditions -- thermally decomposes wood --> char and volatile products form rather than ordinary open-flame combustion alone.`
    - Kind: universal conditional relation. Source: S5, “Combustion of Wood”.
    - Limit: exact charcoal yield and temperature are mechanics/process facts, not LLM-selected numbers.

8. `identified wood under applied cutting, splitting, bending, or bearing load -- responds according to grain, moisture, density and loading mode --> outcome requires a material-and-geometry check.`
    - Kind: universal qualitative mechanics relation. Source: S3, “Factors Affecting Mechanical Properties”.
    - Limit: this is not evidence that a peg, lash, joint, tool, or force exists. Code must establish tool edge/force, wood dimensions and grain, support/restraint, and load before selecting success, damage or failure.

9. `iron surface -- excludes both oxygen and moisture by a protective coating --> iron-corrosion pathway is interrupted.`
   - Kind: universal conditional relation. Source: S6, “Review of Scientific Principles”.
   - Limit: code must establish a continuous protective coating and actual surface coverage; this does not prove that an iron object is coated or undamaged.

10. `iron exposed to weather with oxygen and moisture -- tends to corrode (rust).`
    - Kind: universal conditional relation. Source: S6, “Applications”.
    - Limit: no rate, depth, age or exact corrosion product is inferred; surface state, salts and duration remain code-owned facts.

11. `copper electrically contacting iron in an electrolyte -- does not sacrificially protect the iron because copper is less active than iron.`
    - Kind: bounded galvanic-corrosion relation. Source: S6, “Answers to Questions”, answer 2.
    - Limit: requires actual metal contact and electrolyte; it is not a general claim about copper-alloy patina, colour, or atmospheric-corrosion rate.

12. `wool fibre -- becomes weaker when wet --> load-bearing or pulling result requires its wet condition and actual section to be checked.`
    - Kind: fibre-specific conditional relation. Source: S7, “Natural fibres”, “Wool”.
    - Limit: applies to wool, not linen, hemp, cotton or silk; yarn twist, weave, wear and load remain required facts.

13. `silk fibre -- becomes weaker when wet --> load-bearing or pulling result requires its wet condition and actual section to be checked.`
    - Kind: fibre-specific conditional relation. Source: S7, “Natural fibres”, “Silk”.
    - Limit: applies to silk, not a generic textile; yarn construction, wear and load remain required facts.

14. `plastic clay -- contains adsorbed water between plate-like particles --> particles can slip during shaping.`
    - Kind: universal clay-forming relation. Source: S8, paragraph beginning “Plasticity is the essential feature”.
    - Limit: code must establish clay rather than arbitrary soil, its water state and a shaping action; this does not establish a usable deposit or a finished vessel.

15. `shaped plastic clay -- dries slowly and evenly --> reduces warping and cracking during water-loss shrinkage.`
    - Kind: universal conditional relation. Source: S8, paragraph beginning “Once an object has been molded”.
    - Limit: this is risk reduction, not a guaranteed intact result; thickness, body composition, shape and drying conditions remain material/process facts.

16. `clay containing pore water -- undergoes early kiln heating too quickly --> trapped steam can cause explosion.`
    - Kind: universal conditional relation. Source: S8, paragraph beginning “Further drying occurs during the early stages of kiln firing”.
    - Limit: requires actual pore water and rapid heating; no exact temperature, damage extent or firing outcome is implied.

17. `clay body heated through quartz inversion near 573°C too quickly -- undergoes volume change --> cracking may occur.`
    - Kind: bounded firing-process relation. Source: S8, paragraph beginning “A critical event occurs as the temperature reaches 573°C”.
    - Limit: applies where quartz-bearing clay body and that temperature path are established; it does not prescribe a historical kiln or exact thermal mechanics.

## Runtime use boundary

- These are capability/causality constraints, never presence claims: `material compatible with outcome` does not mean `material exists here`.
- They cannot create historical facts, a workshop, a tool, fuel, a specific species/alloy, a resource quantity, or an actor’s knowledge.
- Exact mass, temperatures, drying duration, joint strength, corrosion extent, damage, inventory consumption and persistence stay code-owned.
- If material identity, causal basis or relevant conditions are unresolved, semantic output should remain conditional or return a data gap; it must not choose a convenient fact.

## Checkable action prerequisites

- **Cut or split wood:** code must know a real target, an edge-capable tool, actor reach, applied force, target grain/dimensions and support. S3 only says these properties affect response; it does not promise a cut.
- **Bend wood or fibre:** code must know actual material identity, cross-section, moisture/condition, bend geometry and restraint. “Wood” or “rope” alone is insufficient.
- **Tie or lash:** code must know a separate flexible fastener, both attachment points, available length and load. No general science claim makes wet cordage universally weaker.
- **Heat or burn wood:** code must know fuel, heat source, oxygen/ventilation, target condition and elapsed process time. S5 does not provide exact ignition or yield values.
- **Corrode iron or couple copper to iron:** code must know actual metals, surface/contact state, moisture/electrolyte and elapsed exposure. S6 does not turn a named metal into a present corrosion state.
- **Load wet textile:** code must know identified fibre, yarn/section, wet condition and load. S7 supports separate wool and silk relations, not a generic “wet cloth is weak” rule.
- **Shape or fire clay:** code must know actual clay body, adsorbed/pore water state, shape/thickness, heat source and heating history. S8 supports no automatic pottery result.
