# Hide and leather processing — bounded historical research

**Status:** candidate research only.  This file neither approves a claim nor
creates a tannery, a hide, a fur, a workshop, a craftsperson, a cutting tool,
or an item of footwear in a scene.

## Existing-corpus boundary

The active pack already distinguishes rawhide, alum-tawed skin, and
vegetable-tanned leather in the CCI water-response material; it also already
has Novgorod footwear, prepared-hide and leather-offcut claims.  Those are not
repeated here.  In particular, a leather form or an offcut is **not** evidence
for the particular tanning method, duration, bath, or recipe that produced it.

This pass found a narrower, independently inspectable historical substrate:
the pre-Mongol Rus archaeological tool corpus describes cutting already-present
leather with named leatherworking tools.  It can support the *post-preparation*
cutting stage, not tanning chemistry.

## Source read

B. A. Kolchin, [*Chernaia metallurgiia i metalloobrabotka v drevnei Rusi
(domongol'skii period)*](https://archaeolog.ru/media/series/mia/MIA_32.pdf),
Moscow: Academy of Sciences USSR, 1953, *Materialy i issledovaniia po
arkheologii SSSR* 32.  The official IA RAS scan was read locally from
`.tmp-pr92-sources/mia-32.pdf` and its text extract.  In the chapter
“Tekhnologiia proizvodstva instrumenta raznykh remesel,” printed pp. 124–125,
the author describes: (a) a cutting knife used to cut leather away from the
worker; (b) a Novgorod example in the figure caption for leather-cutting
knives; (c) a cutter with a vertical wooden handle, also used for cutting
leather; and (d) examined examples designated a leather plane and cutter.
The same passage explains that a shoemaker could cut a leather shape laid on a
board with the palm-supported cutting knife.

Kolchin’s comparative archaeological corpus is broad pre-Mongol Rus.  The
explicit Novgorod knife is direct archaeological material, but the passage is
not a stratified 1230 workshop report and does not identify tanning agents.

Lina Falcão and Maria Eduarda M. Araújo, [“Vegetable Tannins Used in the
Manufacture of Historic Leathers”](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6099987/),
*Molecules* 23 (2018), 1081, was read in full at the publisher/PMC full text.
Its **§1 Introduction** distinguishes a material-science mechanism from a
regional historical assertion: vegetal tannins bind collagen and stabilize an
animal skin against putrefaction; its succinct process account has a previously
washed, limed, dehaired, fleshed and delimed skin treated with crushed vegetal
material or its liquor/maceration, infusion, decoction or extract.  The article
surveys historic European leather and identifies medieval European pit, vat and
bag techniques.  It does **not** demonstrate one of these methods, a given
plant, a recipe or a tannery in Novgorod c. 1230.

## Unclosed part of the gap

The two new tanning candidates close only the causal transformation gap:
prepared hide plus a tannin-bearing plant treatment is a different process
class from rawhide drying, alum tawing, cutting or sewing.  They are not proof
of a local XIII-century tanning facility.  I also read A. V. Kurbatov,
“Кожевенное ремесло лесной зоны Руси в XIII–XIV вв.” (*Stratum plus* 5,
2016, pp. 189–213), via the official IA RAS file: its pp. 189–194 establish the
article's archaeological Old-Rus leather-craft scope and discuss Novgorod
collections, but those pages do not provide a page-specific tanning-agent or
hide-preparation operation.  They are contextual corroboration only, not an
evidence ref for HLP-04/05.

No source opened in this pass supports fur dressing as the same process as
vegetable tanning, nor does it give a c.1230 Novgorod tanning agent or a
recoverable tannery.  Those are deliberately left unasserted.

No candidate says every hide was tanned, that all leather objects had the same
preparation, that fur was tanned leather, or that a specific person/place had
materials, tools, access or specialist knowledge.  Exact resource consumption,
time, physical response and outcome remain code- and state-owned.
