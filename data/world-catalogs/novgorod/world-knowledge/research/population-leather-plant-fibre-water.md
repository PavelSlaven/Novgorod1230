# Leather and plant-fibre water response research

**Status:** research only; no production authoring or approval. Historical
availability of a particular tanning method is outside this note.

## Existing coverage and boundary

`material-response.json` already covers very dry leather becoming stiff and
vulnerable to cracking, damp leather plus mould-favouring conditions, and the
wet-strength response of **wool** and **silk**. The wool/silk premises are not
general textile rules. The candidates below add liquid-water and drying
conditions for leather-processing classes and for plant-cellulosic textile
classes; no candidate asserts a current strap, garment, tanning process, or
water exposure.

2. Renée Dancause, Janet Wagner and Jan Vuori, [*Caring for textiles and costumes*](https://www.canada.ca/en/conservation-institute/services/preventive-conservation/guidelines-collections/textiles-costumes.html), CCI, sections **Natural fibres** and **Water**.

Both complete relevant CCI sections were opened and read through
browser-harness. They are conservation sources for material response, not
evidence of medieval inventories, treatments, or ordinary scene state.

## Candidate premises

| ID | Atomic premise | Exact anchor | Conditions and strict limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| LP-01 | Direct water exposure can damage skins and leathers; the degree depends substantially on processing, finishing, and condition. | CCI leather, “Water,” opening paragraph | A general conditional warning, not a guaranteed damage result for any leather. No water event, tanning type, or object is inferred. |
| LP-02 | Vegetable-tanned leather directly exposed to water may shrink or stiffen; migrating tannins can stain or embrittle its surface. | CCI leather, “Water,” paragraph beginning “Vegetable-tanned leather” | Applies only to that tannage and direct water exposure; no historical availability of vegetable tanning, stain, loss of strength, or existing wet strap follows. |
| LP-03 | Alum-tawed skin has little water resistance because its applied salts can be washed out, producing a hard condition. | CCI leather, “Alum-tawed skin”; “Water” | A tannage-specific conditional material response, not a claim that alum-tawing was locally available or that any pale hide is alum-tawed. |
| LP-04 | Rawhide can swell when sufficiently wet and shrink on drying. | CCI leather, “Rawhide” | Rawhide is not interchangeable with tanned leather; putrefaction, strong contraction, and named object examples are excluded. Historical rawhide-lacing availability needs separate evidence. |
| PF-01 | Plant textile fibres are cellulosic; the class includes flax/linen and hemp among other bast/plant fibres. | CCI textiles, “Natural fibres,” paragraphs 1–2 | Taxonomic bridge only. Cellulosic does not supply one universal wet-strength, shrinkage, or permeability rule. |
| PF-02 | New linen made from flax is strong, absorbent, quick-drying, and low-resiliency. | CCI textiles, “Natural fibres,” flax/linen paragraph | Linen-specific, new-material description. It does not fix behaviour of aged linen, hemp, woven construction, wet tensile strength, or drying time. |
| PF-03 | Excess water can penetrate natural textile fibres and make them swell; textile structure and material state still govern the result. | CCI textiles, “Water,” paragraph beginning “Excess water” | This is not a claim that every fibre gets wet, every wet textile swells, or every porous/wettable fabric transmits water through. |
| PF-04 | Water damage to a textile can include shrinkage, loss of flexibility, stretching, and tears when water weight exceeds what fibres can support. | CCI textiles, “Water,” opening paragraph | Conditional textile-object outcome; no individual fibre type, wet strength, exact load, damage, or irreversible outcome is established. |
| PF-05 | Water and oils can carry soil into textile fibre structure by capillary action; later oxidation can weaken or break fibres. | CCI textiles, “Pollutants,” paragraph beginning “Liquid contaminants” | Requires a contaminating liquid and time-dependent subsequent chemistry. It does not assert liquid ingress for every textile or immediate damage. |

## Held distinctions

Processing changes leather's water resistance; rawhide, alum-tawed skin,
vegetable-tanned leather, and oil-/mineral-tanned products cannot be collapsed
into one response. Likewise, CCI identifies hemp and linen as plant-cellulosic
but supplies a direct absorbency statement only for linen. No broad “all bast
fibres are stronger/weaker when wet” candidate is proposed.
