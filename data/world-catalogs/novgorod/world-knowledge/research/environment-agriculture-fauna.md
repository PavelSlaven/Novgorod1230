# Novgorod 1230: environment, agriculture, fauna — research candidates

**Status:** research-only candidate corpus; not a runtime profile, presence table, recipe list, or authority to materialize anything.
**Scope:** Novgorod land, c. 1230. The nearest direct evidence is often dated only broadly to medieval Novgorod (normally tenth–fifteenth centuries); such evidence is deliberately not recast as an observation made in the year 1230.

## Source register

- **S1 — Hamilton-Dyer, Brisbane & Maltby (2017),** “Fish, feather, fur and forest: Exploitation of wild animals in medieval Novgorod and its territory”, *Quaternary International* 460, 97–107. The article explicitly concerns medieval Novgorod and its territory; DOI and publication metadata: [10.1016/j.quaint.2016.04.024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.quaint.2016.04.024).
- **S2 — Maltby & Brisbane (eds., 2020),** *Animals and Archaeology in Northern Medieval Russia: Zooarchaeological Studies in Novgorod and its Region*. This is a regional zooarchaeological synthesis, not a claim that each animal was present in every 1230 scene: [10.2307/j.ctv138wsxr](https://doi.org/10.2307/j.ctv138wsxr).
- **S3 — Zinoviev (2025),** “When Synanthropic Birds Appeared in Medieval Novgorod the Great and Tver (Russia): Historical and Zooarchaeological Accounts”, *International Journal of Osteoarchaeology* 35, 89–96. Its abstract distinguishes species attested by the thirteenth century from later colonizers: [10.1002/oa.70009](https://doi.org/10.1002/oa.70009).
- **S4 — Volgina (2024),** “Agricultural implements of medieval Novgorod: systematization, chronology”, *Povolzhskaya Arkheologiya* 48, 43–60. It is an archaeological study of the tool assemblage; it must not be read as a crop calendar for a particular holding: [10.24852/pa2024.2.48](https://doi.org/10.24852/pa2024.2.48).
- **S5 — Spiridonova & Aleshinskaya (2012),** “Results of palynological investigations of the archaeological sites in the Lake Ilmen and Lake Kubenskoye study areas”, in *The Archaeology of Medieval Novgorod in Context*. This is the cited pollen source for a documented medieval increase in meadowland in the local regional synthesis: [book record](https://doi.org/10.2307/j.ctvh1dqcg) (the source is listed in the bibliography of S1).
- **S6 — Serezhnikova (2024),** “Berry and fruit plants in the nutrition of Novgorod dwellers in the Middle Ages”, *Environmental Dynamics and Global Climate Change*. The stated object is plant macroremains from medieval Novgorod; use for urban consumption/material evidence, not for a universal rural crop list: [journal article record](https://edgccjournal.org/view/).

## Candidate records

### ENV-01 — waterlogged urban archaeological context

- **Period / region / location envelope:** medieval Veliky Novgorod, including the city’s waterlogged archaeological deposits; relevant to excavation-backed urban evidence, not to every part of Novgorod land.
- **Candidate:** anaerobic, waterlogged deposits can preserve organic material exceptionally well, so urban plant and animal assemblages are unusually informative but also site- and recovery-method-specific.
- **Qualifiers:** this describes the evidential context, not a universal terrain modifier or proof that an animal/plant occurs at a particular unexcavated location. S1 explicitly frames Novgorod’s excavated medieval deposits as exceptionally preserved anaerobic material.
- **Evidence / confidence:** [S1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.quaint.2016.04.024); **high** for the excavated-city preservation context.
- **Exclusions / conflicts:** do not infer a city-wide swamp, fixed ground moisture, or rural availability from preservation conditions alone.
- **Gameplay use:** contextualize why a discovered urban refuse layer may retain organic remains; never instantiate food, livestock, fish, or fuel merely from this candidate.

### ENV-02 — managed open land within a forest-and-water landscape

- **Period / region / location envelope:** Lake Ilmen region / Novgorod hinterland, medieval proxy; suitable only for a rural scene whose canonical geography already places it in the Ilmen basin or comparable local landscape.
- **Candidate:** pollen evidence records a significant medieval increase in meadowland, consistent with a managed mosaic of open land, hay/meadow use, woodland, and waters rather than an undifferentiated wilderness.
- **Qualifiers:** “meadowland” is a landscape signal, not proof of a meadow at a named scene, its ownership, grazing load, or a particular field system. S5 is palynological and regional; it does not resolve an exact 1230 micro-location.
- **Evidence / confidence:** [S5](https://doi.org/10.2307/j.ctvh1dqcg), as cited by the Novgorod regional archaeological synthesis; **medium**.
- **Exclusions / conflicts:** no fixed percentages of forest, pasture, or arable; no claim of universal soil fertility; do not turn the proxy into a canonical route or land parcel.
- **Gameplay use:** an admissible factual backdrop for context-sensitive descriptions or candidate queries after spatial/materialization owners establish the rural location; not a presence grant.

### ENV-03 — inland water as a resource context, not an inexhaustible pantry

- **Period / region / location envelope:** medieval Novgorod and its territory; rivers, Lake Ilmen-connected waters, and other already-authorized freshwater settings.
- **Candidate:** wild-animal exploitation in the regional archaeological record includes fish alongside mammals and birds; S1’s title, scope, and source bibliography include a dedicated study of fish in medieval Novgorod.
- **Qualifiers:** biology makes freshwater habitats plausible; historical use is supported only at the regional scale here. Catch, species, season, access, nets/boats, and quantity remain separate questions for the relevant source, property, weather, and process owners.
- **Evidence / confidence:** [S1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.quaint.2016.04.024); **medium** for regional exploitation, **low** for any exact water body without a local source.
- **Exclusions / conflicts:** no automatic fish stock, ice condition, boat, fishing right, or successful catch; do not use a generic “river” to create a canonical water connection.
- **Gameplay use:** allows a knowledge slice to explain why fishing is historically compatible in an established freshwater context; it cannot resolve the attempt itself.

### AGR-01 — agricultural work is archaeologically attested, but tools are not a crop whitelist

- **Period / region / location envelope:** medieval Novgorod; principally farm, yard, storage, and settlement contexts where an already-existing tool or work process is evidenced.
- **Candidate:** a substantial archaeological assemblage of medieval Novgorod agricultural implements has been systematized and dated by S4, supporting agriculture as a real regional practice.
- **Qualifiers:** S4 supports the existence and chronology of implements, not that every household owns a plough/harrow/sickle, nor that a player may obtain one without an owner/source.
- **Evidence / confidence:** [S4](https://doi.org/10.24852/pa2024.2.48); **medium-high** for the regional practice and excavated tool evidence.
- **Exclusions / conflicts:** no prescribed inventory, no guarantee of iron tool availability in 1230, and no automatic inference of a particular crop from a tool.
- **Gameplay use:** ground ordinary agricultural activity in a suitable established settlement; resolve exact tool, access, labour, season, and yield through existing owners.

### AGR-02 — cereals are a historical crop class, not a single annual outcome

- **Period / region / location envelope:** medieval Novgorod / its agrarian hinterland; a cultivated plot, granary, or food context must already be causally grounded.
- **Candidate:** the regional archaeological synthesis reports cultivated cereals including barley, wheat, rye, and possibly oats in the Novgorod context. The statement is a synthesis-level crop class, not a field-by-field inventory.[^cereals]
- **Qualifiers:** crop identifications must retain the source’s uncertainty (“possibly oats”); this record does not settle which cereal grew on a particular plot in 1230, cultivar, sowing date, crop rotation, yield, or whether grain was local rather than traded.
- **Evidence / confidence:** [S5](https://doi.org/10.2307/j.ctvh1dqcg) regional synthesis and its archaeological source base; **medium**.
- **Exclusions / conflicts:** no “main crop” ranking, no deterministic winter/spring calendar, no potato/maize/tomato, and no claim that cereal remains prove local cultivation rather than storage/consumption/trade.
- **Gameplay use:** answer a bounded historical-compatibility query after place and causal source are known; use weather/time/process mechanics for germination, harvest, spoilage, and labour.

### AGR-03 — berries and fruit plants as macroremain-backed food context

- **Period / region / location envelope:** medieval urban Novgorod deposits and food-use contexts; a rural wild-fruit or orchard scene requires its own local evidence.
- **Candidate:** S6 studies plant macroremains to investigate berry and fruit plants in medieval Novgorodians’ diet, supporting a broad food-use context for such plant remains.
- **Qualifiers:** consumption evidence does not decide whether a fruit was locally gathered, garden-grown, preserved, or imported; neither botanical possibility nor a name in dialogue proves a standing plant is present.
- **Evidence / confidence:** [S6](https://edgccjournal.org/view/); **medium** for medieval urban dietary use, **low** for a specific species/location pending extract-level review.
- **Exclusions / conflicts:** no invented orchard, no species list promoted from model memory, and no fixed ripening date.
- **Gameplay use:** permit a carefully qualified food-context fact in an already-grounded urban household/refuse context; materialization still needs a source and season.

### FAU-01 — domestic livestock should be modeled as managed, not ambient wildlife

- **Period / region / location envelope:** medieval Novgorod and its region; established household, byre, market, transport, or refuse contexts.
- **Candidate:** S2 is a dedicated regional zooarchaeological corpus on human-animal relations. Its use supports domestic-animal evidence as managed economic context rather than treating cattle, pigs, sheep/goats, horses, or dogs as ambient scenery.
- **Qualifiers:** exact taxon proportions, sex/age, ownership, traction use, and live presence are unresolved without a narrower extract and a world source. “Domestic” is historical practice/relationship, not a biological capability claim.
- **Evidence / confidence:** [S2](https://doi.org/10.2307/j.ctv138wsxr); **medium** for regional domestic-animal context, **low** for a named animal in 1230.
- **Exclusions / conflicts:** do not create a herd, dairy product, mount, or butchered meat from this record; no modern improved breeds or veterinary assumptions.
- **Gameplay use:** contextual framing for a source-backed candidate query; existing item/property, body, time, and resource owners decide all concrete consequences.

### FAU-02 — wild mammals: food, fur, and forest use are distinct questions

- **Period / region / location envelope:** medieval Novgorod and its territory, especially an already-established forest/wetland/water-edge or trade context.
- **Candidate:** S1 finds wild mammals made only a small dietary contribution in the Novgorodian assemblages it discusses; hare, beaver, and elk are reported as the most frequent wild-mammal bones, while very few fur-bearing mammals occur in the faunal assemblage.
- **Qualifiers:** a scarcity of fur-bearing bones does **not** show that fur trade was absent; it cautions against using food-bone frequency as a proxy for trade volume. Species occurrence in the regional corpus is neither a map of every forest nor a guarantee of a hunt.
- **Evidence / confidence:** [S1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.quaint.2016.04.024); **high** for the article’s reported assemblage pattern, **medium** for application to the c.1230 regional setting.
- **Exclusions / conflicts:** no bear/wolf/beaver encounter table, no guaranteed pelt, no assumption that hunting success follows biological habitat alone.
- **Gameplay use:** distinguish a food attempt from fur procurement and from an observed animal. A forest scene may query compatibility; it may not materialize prey or trade goods without causal basis.

### FAU-03 — birds at the urban edge require taxon- and period-sensitive treatment

- **Period / region / location envelope:** medieval Novgorod city and immediately human-modified urban habitat.
- **Candidate:** S3 finds corvids and pigeons among early urban adapters; feral pigeons and Eurasian tree sparrows are documented by the thirteenth century, while magpies and starlings are treated as later-medieval colonizers.
- **Qualifiers:** the paper itself stresses recovery and identification biases, including the difficulty of separating genuine synanthropes from seasonal visitors or raptor prey. Thus the candidate is stronger for “historically compatible urban birds” than for an exact everyday flock.
- **Evidence / confidence:** [S3](https://doi.org/10.1002/oa.70009); **high** for the stated thirteenth-century distinction, **medium** for a specific scene.
- **Exclusions / conflicts:** do not place magpies or starlings in 1230 merely because they are familiar modern urban birds; no automatic nesting, trapping, or pest behaviour.
- **Gameplay use:** improve historically bounded narration/knowledge answers after a city scene exists; behaviour, presence, and capture remain separate causal decisions.

### FAU-04 — raptors and falconry are attested practices, not default countryside decoration

- **Period / region / location envelope:** medieval Novgorod and its territory; a person, equipment set, or trained bird must already be authoritatively established.
- **Candidate:** S1 treats birds and exploitation in the Novgorod regional record and cites archaeological work on falconry; this supports historical compatibility of falconry as a practice in the wider territory.
- **Qualifiers:** the evidence does not warrant assigning a falcon, hawk, jesses, training knowledge, or elite status to an arbitrary NPC.
- **Evidence / confidence:** [S1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.quaint.2016.04.024); **medium** for regional attestation, **low** for any individual or exact 1230 venue.
- **Exclusions / conflicts:** no species-specific performance stats, no universal hunting method, no free trained raptor.
- **Gameplay use:** a narrow historical-context answer for an already-authored/causally grounded falconry situation; not a candidate generator.

### SEA-01 — season must remain a contextual process, not a frozen medieval calendar

- **Period / region / location envelope:** any Novgorod-land outdoor scene around 1230.
- **Candidate:** the agricultural, pollen, plant-remain, and faunal sources establish seasonal-dependent activities and environmental evidence but do not provide an exact day-by-day weather or phenology record for 1230 (S1, S4–S6).
- **Qualifiers:** universal biology supports seasonal variation; historical availability/practice must still be tied to the regional evidence and committed local conditions. A date alone does not prove ground state, navigable ice, ripeness, fodder, migration, or harvest completion.
- **Evidence / confidence:** **high** for the evidential limitation; no false precision asserted.
- **Exclusions / conflicts:** prohibit fixed weather/harvest/ripening tables derived from this research shard; no use of modern climate normals as facts of 1230.
- **Gameplay use:** require time/weather/process owners to resolve concrete seasonal effects; World Knowledge may state only qualified historical compatibility.

### SOI-01 — soils: do not manufacture a pan-regional soil fact

- **Period / region / location envelope:** Novgorod land generally.
- **Candidate:** **no affirmative soil-type claim promoted.** The examined sources support landscape, agriculture, pollen, and assemblage contexts, but do not establish a safely localizable thirteenth-century soil classification for the whole Novgorod land (S1, S4, S5).
- **Qualifiers:** soil texture, drainage, fertility, and field productivity are location-specific and cannot be inferred from “Novgorod” or from an agricultural implement.
- **Evidence / confidence:** **high** for this exclusion based on the present source set.
- **Exclusions / conflicts:** no universal “Novgorod soil”, no yield modifier, and no claim that any canonical location has arable ground unless a spatial/local source supplies it.
- **Gameplay use:** return a bounded `unresolved`/data-gap for exact soil questions rather than a model-memory fallback.

## Cross-cutting conflicts and authoring rules

- **Biology ≠ historical availability.** A species can be biologically plausible today but not evidenced for the historical, regional, and location envelope. Conversely, a bone, macroremain, or pollen signal demonstrates a past context but not live current presence in a player scene.
- **Regional medieval evidence ≠ 1230 observation.** Candidates marked medium are historical compatibility evidence; use a narrower temporal/local source before promoting them to an exact 1220–1240 claim.
- **Consumption ≠ production; remains ≠ ownership.** Urban plant and animal remains can derive from gathering, cultivation, import, storage, refuse, craft, or trade. Do not convert them into a local source, a farm, or an inventory.
- **Absence in an assemblage ≠ impossibility.** Recovery, preservation, and disposal biases matter, especially for birds (S3) and traded furs (S1).
- **No anachronistic defaulting.** Do not use potatoes, maize, tomatoes, modern improved livestock breeds, mechanised agriculture, modern veterinary practice, or a modern urban-bird assemblage as ordinary 1230 assumptions.
