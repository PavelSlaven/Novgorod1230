## Dwelling interior

An inhabited room can combine rest, work, storage, and passage differently as household size, means, weather, care, and maintenance change. Benches or platforms with bedding may keep the walls, while a stove or hearth takes part of the room and leaves work space and passage around it; containers and bundles can be shifted as needed rather than treated as mandatory furniture. Daylight from an opening leaves farther corners darker, while a hearth or small available flame changes evening use. Opening a way for smoke also brings draft or rain. Tasks can move toward light, a bench, a doorway, or the yard, and can be shared or delayed when space, heat, smoke, fatigue, or care make the usual arrangement awkward.

Wet footwear, carried water, cooking, and routine work can leave temporary moisture, marks, and smells; later traffic, drying, and cleaning can change them.
