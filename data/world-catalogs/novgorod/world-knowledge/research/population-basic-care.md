# Basic care and sanitation — bounded causal research

**Status:** research candidates only; no self-approval or production edit.
This is a narrow universal care/sanitation substrate for an already-existing
wound.  It is not medical advice, a diagnosis, a treatment protocol, evidence
of actor competence, a historical Rus' practice, or a guarantee of healing.

## Sources independently opened

1. U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, [“Guidelines for Personal
   Hygiene During an Emergency”](https://www.cdc.gov/water-emergency/safety/guidelines-for-personal-hygiene-during-an-emergency.html),
   section “Protect wounds,” updated 2024.  Full section opened on 2026-09-04.
   It says that clean, open wounds should be covered with a waterproof bandage
   to reduce the chance of infection; the immediately preceding text confines
   this to a contaminated-water emergency context.
2. U.S. Centers for Disease Control and Prevention, [“Preventing
   Diabetes-Related Amputations”](https://www.cdc.gov/diabetes/diabetes-complications/preventing-diabetes-related-amputations.html),
   subsection “What to do when you get a cut on your foot,” updated 2024.
   Full subsection opened on 2026-09-04.  It directs applying direct pressure
   with a clean bandage or cloth to control bleeding, then rinsing with clean
   water and covering the cleaned wound.  Its page context is diabetic foot
   care; only the qualitative pressure/bleeding relation is proposed below.

## Composition boundary

These candidates remain domain-internal causal possibilities.  They may inform
resolution only when the wound, relevant clean material/water or protective
cover, access, and attempt are independently established.  They must not
create a wound, clean textile, bandage, water source, medical role, recovery,
or an outcome.  No numerical timing, dosage, prognosis, or player-facing
health instruction follows from this research.
