# Terrain and ground-source research

**Status:** research only; no production authoring or approval disposition.

## Scope and duplicate boundary

`wk:env:terrain` already has independent coverage for buried soil,
waterlogged archaeological layers, the Volkhov--Ilmen woodland/meadow setting,
soil infiltration, and hydric soils. This note therefore does not repeat those
relations. It investigates only the broader geological basis of ground
materials in the Novgorod region. Geological occurrence is not evidence for a
1230 quarry, pit, stock, canonical landform, or an actor's permission to dig,
remove, build with, or cross a particular material.

## Source fully read

N. G. Dmitruk and M. P. Druzhnova, [*Soil areas of the Novgorod region in the
context of their agricultural use*](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1755-1315/613/1/012025),
*IOP Conference Series: Earth and Environmental Science* 613 (2020) 012025,
DOI [10.1088/1755-1315/613/1/012025](https://doi.org/10.1088/1755-1315/613/1/012025),
pp. 1--7, open-access PDF. The complete article was read. It is a modern
regional soil-geography study by Yaroslav-the-Wise Novgorod State University;
its present-day regional descriptions are geological corroboration, not direct
evidence of medieval extraction or land use.

The authors state in the abstract and introduction that Quaternary parent
rocks and modern sediments are the principal soil-forming factors in the
region. They specify glacial, fluvioglacial, and lacustrine-glacial parent
materials; the relevant ground classes include boulder and sandy loams,
fluvioglacial sands with gravel and pebbles, and lacustrine-glacial sands and
clays. They separately describe lake-alluvial and alluvial material in the
Ilmen--Volkhov floodplain and river valleys.

## Non-candidates and remaining gap

The article discusses agriculture, drainage, yields, and reclamation, but those
are modern applied conclusions and are not candidates for medieval production.
It supplies no historical record of quarrying, digging, aggregate collection,
clay winning, stone source ownership, or construction procurement. Accordingly
the extractable-source portion of the coverage gap remains open even if the
regional geological candidates are independently approved. A separate
archaeological or documentary source would be required before authoring a
historical extraction relation.
