## Candidate premises

### 1. A feathered, mobile youngster on the ground can be a fledgling rather than an abandoned bird

- **Support.** [Wildlife Center of Virginia, *Baby Bird Season Is Here: What to Do When You Find a Young Bird*](https://wildlifecenter.org/news-events/news/2026/baby-bird-season-here-what-do-when-you-find-young-bird), section “Fledglings: Meant to be on the ground,” distinguishes a fully feathered bird able to hop or flutter as likely fledgling, says time on the ground can be normal, and recommends space while keeping pets and children away. [Ottawa Valley Wild Bird Care Centre, *Bird Emergency*](https://www.wildbirdcarecentre.org/bird-emergency), section “What do I do if I see a fledgling bird on the ground or alone?”, likewise describes fully feathered, alert birds able to walk, hop, or perch low as birds that should be left alone.
- **Proposed claim wording.**
  - RU: «Полностью или почти полностью оперившаяся молодая птица, которая выглядит бодрой и может ходить, прыгать либо держаться на низкой опоре, может быть слётком: само её пребывание на земле ещё не доказывает сиротство или травму. Для такой птицы обычно важнее оставить пространство и убрать непосредственное беспокойство, чем забирать её.»
  - EN: “A fully or mostly feathered young bird that appears alert and can walk, hop, or use low cover may be a fledgling: being on the ground alone does not itself prove orphaning or injury. Giving it space and removing immediate disturbance is ordinarily more appropriate than taking it away.”
- **Limits.** These signs do not identify a species or prove health, parental care, nest location, or a particular response. “Usually” does not cover a bird with an obvious injury or immediate danger.

### 2. Sparse plumage and direct danger change the boundary, but do not authorize invented care

- **Support.** The Wildlife Center of Virginia page above, section “Nestlings: Help them get back home,” states that a bird with few or no feathers is a nestling and should be returned to its nest if possible. [Wildlife Education and Rehabilitation Center, *Found A Baby Bird*](https://www.werc-ca.org/foundababybird), “Baby Birds,” says a featherless or barely feathered nestling found outside its nest needs return if possible, while a feathered fledgling should be left alone unless injured; if cats are around, remove the cats or move the bird only within a short radius of where it was found. [Scottish SPCA, *Lone Baby Animal Advice*](https://www.scottishspca.org/advice/lone-babies/), “What should I do if I find a baby bird on the ground?”, says a healthy fledgling should be left alone absent immediate dangers, but can be moved a short distance to safety when on a road or vulnerable to nearby predators.
- **Proposed claim wording.**
  - RU: «Почти голый или слабо оперившийся птенец вне гнезда и молодая птица в непосредственной опасности требуют иной осторожности: сначала по возможности устраняют опасность; при неизбежном коротком переносе не отделяют птицу далеко от найденного места и не выдают это за лечение. Если есть доступное гнездо, птенца можно вернуть туда; иначе границы дальнейшего ухода не следует придумывать.»
  - EN: “A nearly bare or sparsely feathered chick outside a nest, and a young bird in immediate danger, call for different caution: remove the danger where possible; if a brief move is unavoidable, do not take the bird far from where it was found or treat that move as treatment. If an accessible nest is known, the chick may be returned; otherwise the limits of further care should not be invented.”
- **Limits.** No distance, handling technique, feeding, watering, warming, rehabilitation, nest construction, diagnosis, or survival outcome is asserted. A shrub, nest, predator, injury, and parental reaction remain specific scene facts rather than consequences of this claim.

### 3. Brief examination of domestic fowl can secure wings while supporting the whole body, without compressing the breast

- **Support.** [Merck Veterinary Manual, *Physical Examination of Backyard Poultry*](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/exotic-and-laboratory-animals/backyard-poultry/physical-examination-of-backyard-poultry), section “Physical Examination of Backyard Poultry,” advises holding wings folded close to the body, supporting the breast when lifting domestic fowl, avoiding upside-down restraint, and not squeezing the breast because thoracic compression compromises breathing. [Penn State Extension, *Handling Poultry*](https://extension.psu.edu/handling-poultry), handling demonstration text, says to restrain wings, lift the whole body rather than wings alone, support the bird with one arm, and cover wings with the other to prevent flapping injury.
- **Proposed claim wording.**
  - RU: «При кратком осмотре домашней птицы тело поддерживают целиком, а крылья удерживают сложенными у тела, не поднимая птицу за одно крыло. Удержание не должно сдавливать грудь или мешать дыхательным движениям; если птица сильно сопротивляется, спокойствие и прекращение лишней возни важнее продолжения осмотра.»
  - EN: “For a brief examination of domestic fowl, support the whole body and keep the wings folded close to it rather than lifting the bird by one wing. Restraint must not squeeze the breast or obstruct breathing movements; when the bird struggles strongly, calm handling and ending unnecessary manipulation take priority over continuing the inspection.”
- **Limits.** This is not a species-specific restraint protocol, force threshold, duration, wing test, diagnosis, treatment, or assurance that bird or handler will be unharmed.

### 4. Observable emergency signs are a boundary for casual inspection, not a diagnosis

- **Support.** Merck Veterinary Manual, *Physical Examination of Backyard Poultry*, paragraphs immediately before “To restrain chickens and other domestic fowl for examination,” lists acute hemorrhage, head trauma, seizures or other neurologic signs, open fractures, extreme respiratory difficulty, and weakness as signs requiring emergency care before examination. Its earlier observation paragraph gives increased respiratory effort (including tail bobbing), unusual posture, drooping wings, and head tilt as abnormal findings.
- **Proposed claim wording.**
  - RU: «Кровотечение, явная открытая переломная травма, крайняя трудность дыхания, выраженная слабость или тяжёлые необычные позы — наблюдаемые основания прекратить обычный осмотр и искать более опытную помощь. Торчащее перо или неохотный взмах сами по себе такого вывода не дают и не называют причину.»
  - EN: “Bleeding, an apparent open fracture, extreme breathing difficulty, marked weakness, or severe abnormal posture are observable reasons to stop ordinary examination and seek more experienced help. A projecting feather or reluctant wingbeat alone does not establish that conclusion or its cause.”
- **Limits.** “More experienced help” is a gameplay-safe boundary, not a claim that a particular medieval specialist, treatment, contagion, cause, or outcome exists. The source’s clinical terms must not be converted into a diagnosis from one ambiguous sign.
