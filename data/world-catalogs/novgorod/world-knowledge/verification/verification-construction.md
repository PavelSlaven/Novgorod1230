## Verdicts

- A01–A04: APPROVE_WITH_LIMITS. The excavated X-century bridge had log-crib
  supports; the described support form is a polygonal log frame, lowered onto
  the riverbed as courses were added and filled with stones. The observed
  pentagonal form is not mandatory for every crib or bridge. Broad medieval
  compatibility is inferred/medium, not a 1230 structure or capacity.
- A05–A07: do not promote in this gameplay subset. Specimen log-course counts
  and reconstructed exact spans add no needed general causal relation; A06's
  form duplicates A02/A04. This is scope/duplication, not factual rejection.
- B01: APPROVE_WITH_LIMITS. Orlov describes a sternpost seated in the keel's
  corresponding recess, joined with a wooden peg and iron nails. Keep the
  documented joint type, not a requirement for all boats.
- B02: reuse B01's peg-joint premise. Do not confuse the lower peg hole with
  the separate transverse hole interpreted in connection with a rudder.
- B03: APPROVE. Forged broad-headed iron nails with quadrangular sections are
  directly described in the observed ship timber.
- B04: APPROVE_WITH_LIMITS. Drilled holes in the plank support Orlov's
  reconstruction of wooden-nail attachment to side frames.
- B05–B06: APPROVE_WITH_LIMITS, inferred/medium. Tarred tow in plank gaps,
  pressed by battens held with iron clamps, is Orlov's interpretation by
  comparison with contemporary local thick-planked boats, not directly
  preserved medieval caulking demonstrated by this report. Retain this
  distinction in runtime wording and evidence.
- B07: reuse the existing approved repair-context relation; no duplicate.
