## Scope

Four small claims fill two static gaps: qualitative limits for wet parchment
and inked records, plus modern universal first-aid limits for minor thermal
burns. The medical sources are not an attestation of medical practice in
Novgorod in 1230. All claims preserve material variability, concrete-scene
uncertainty, and qualified-care escalation rather than prescribe restoration or
treatment.

## Sources read

1. Canadian Conservation Institute, [*Basic Care of Books*](https://www.canada.ca/en/conservation-institute/services/conservation-preservation-publications/canadian-conservation-institute-notes/basic-care-books.html), “Materials: Parchment”, accessed 2026-09-05. Parchment/vellum should not become wet; wetting risks cockling, distortion and, in degraded parchment, irreversible gelatinization.
2. U.S. National Park Service, [*Conserve O Gram 13/2*](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/conserve-o-gram-13-2-flatten-folded-rolled-paper.htm), “Caution!”, accessed 2026-09-05. Parchment, vellum, iron-gall ink and poor-condition items require professional-conservator expertise for controlled humidification and flattening.
3. NHS, [*Burns and scalds*](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/burns-and-scalds/), accessed 2026-09-05. Covers prompt cool-water cooling, no removal of adherent material, no creams/oils/butter, and modern emergency red flags.

## Candidate boundaries

- Parchment/ink response is material- and media-sensitive; professional
  treatment limit is retained rather than inferred as an exact procedure.
- Burn claims are modern universal biology and escalation context only. They do
  not diagnose a person, establish a medieval healer's method, or create a
  recovery result.
