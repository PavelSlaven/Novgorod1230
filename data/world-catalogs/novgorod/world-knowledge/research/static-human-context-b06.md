# Static human context B06 — research only

**Scope:** candidate general physiology/context premises for a historical game.
They are not player diagnosis, body-state assertion, exact mechanical effect,
medical protocol, treatment or dosage.

## Checked production bundle

`production-v1/runtime-bundle.json` contains 1,295 claims. It has general
surface-friction and separate consciousness/vomiting premises, but no premise
linking skin friction to blisters/abrasion, no post-head-injury warning set,
and no near-work/visual-fatigue or dim-light non-harm premise. Existing cold,
wet-clothing, warmth and rest coverage was not duplicated.

## Candidate premises

1. **Repeated rubbing can damage skin and may produce a friction blister.**
   A protective covering and removal of the rubbing source are general
   protective context while the area heals.
   Source: [NHS — Blisters](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/blisters/).
   **Limit:** no particular wound, severity, infection, recovery time, actor
   state, material, treatment procedure or outcome.

2. **After a head injury, worsening or reduced consciousness, repeated
   vomiting, or a seizure are warning observations that call for urgent
   qualified help.**
   Sources: [NHS — Head injury and concussion](https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/head-injury-and-concussion/);
   [University Hospitals Sussex — Adult head injury](https://www.uhsussex.nhs.uk/resources/copy-of-adult-head-injury-ed-leaflet/).
   **Limit:** no single observation diagnoses concussion, bleeding, brain
   injury or any cause; no prognosis, exact escalation protocol, treatment,
   transport rule or individual outcome.

3. **Sustained visually demanding near work can be associated with temporary
   eye fatigue or discomfort; pauses and suitable illumination can support
   visual comfort.**
   Sources: [NHS — Dry eyes](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/dry-eyes/);
   [Moorfields Eye Hospital — visual comfort](https://www.moorfields.nhs.uk/children-and-young-people/paediatric-low-vision-assessment/visual-comfort).
   **Limit:** no visual diagnosis, acuity, fatigue threshold, task duration,
   individual performance effect or required regimen.

4. **Reading in dim light may make eyes feel tired, but dim-light reading is
   not itself harmful to eyes.**
   Source: [NIH National Eye Institute — Healthy Vision](https://www.nei.nih.gov/sites/default/files/2019-06/NEI_Healthy-Vision_booklet_WEB_508%20%281%29.pdf), “Eye Care: Myths and Facts”.
   **Limit:** this does not rule out eye disease, injury, existing visual
   difficulty, glare discomfort, or any scene-specific visibility condition.
