## Candidate general premises

1. **European insectivorous-bat context.** British bats are active at night,
   find their way and catch food with echolocation, and are insectivorous. This
   can make a bat's dusk flight, insect-foraging and calling context plausible
   when a bat is otherwise independently established. It does not extend to all
   bats globally or identify species, number, sex, roost, prey capture, location
   or an actual current animal. [London Borough of Bromley, *Bromley
   biodiversity species action plan: bats*](https://www.bromley.gov.uk/conservation/bromley-biodiversity-species-action-plan-bats-species)

2. **Roost and season qualification.** British bats need summer roosts and
   undisturbed winter hibernation sites. Day/night and season may therefore
   shape a description of an already-established bat or roost; they do not prove
   that any cavity, building or cave contains bats. This is a British bat context,
   not an all-bat rule. [London Borough of Bromley, *Bromley biodiversity species
   action plan: bats*](https://www.bromley.gov.uk/conservation/bromley-biodiversity-species-action-plan-bats-species)

3. **Cold-period qualification.** In cool climates most bats hibernate during
   cold weather, while cool temperatures can reduce activity. A winter or cold
   setting therefore constrains likely activity, but does not establish torpor,
   species, duration, survival, or absence of every bat. [UF/IFAS, *Conservation
   of Bats in Florida*](https://ask.ifas.ufl.edu/publication/UW291/)

4. **Roost disturbance is consequential.** Many bat species may abandon a
   roost after minimal human disturbance; disturbance can arouse hibernating
   bats and consume stored energy. A game may therefore favour distance,
   quietness and observing an exit from outside over entering or provoking a
   roost. This is an outcome-risk premise, not a capture, exclusion, treatment,
   season, mortality or population prediction. [USDA Forest Service, *Bat Roost
   Etiquette*](https://www.fs.usda.gov/Internet/FSE_DOCUMENTS/fseprd899352.pdf)

5. **Non-contact exit observation.** A bat that enters a structure will often
   locate an exit and leave on its own; wildlife guidance recommends remaining
   quiet, keeping distance and not touching it. Such a scene may permit waiting
   and observation rather than forcing a direct interaction. It does not make
   departure guaranteed, supply a removal procedure, or create a modern
   rehabilitator/health service in the medieval world. [California Department of
   Fish and Wildlife, *Human-Wildlife Conflicts: Bats*](https://wildlife.ca.gov/HWC/Bats)

6. **Observable mobility change is not a cause.** In equids, a changed gait or
   reduced performance can be noticed by a handler, rider or caregiver; the
   source/cause of lameness normally needs veterinary examination and can include
   pain, mechanical dysfunction or neurologic conditions. Narration may retain
   a visible uneven step, reluctance or reduced weight-bearing as an observation,
   but must not infer lesion, pain severity, body part, disease or treatment.
   [USDA APHIS, *Equine 2015: Lameness Occurrence and Management*](https://www.aphis.usda.gov/sites/default/files/eq2015_rept3.pdf)

7. **Animal-welfare indicators need species and context.** Pain, distress or
   suffering may be monitored through welfare indicators, but those indicators
   must be established for the species being assessed. Reduced activity,
   abnormal posture or a behaviour change are therefore cues for further
   attention, not interchangeable proof of pain, fatigue, fear, thirst or a
   diagnosis across animals. [USDA National Agricultural Library, *Animal
   Welfare Assessments*](https://www.nal.usda.gov/animal-health-and-welfare/animal-welfare-assessments)

8. **Working-horse heat/load context and overlapping cues.** Heat, humidity,
   direct sun, poor ventilation, prolonged/excessive work and transport are
   relevant context for equine heat strain; rapid breathing, weakness,
   incoordination, dull behaviour, heavy sweating or refusal to work are
   observable possible signs. These observations can justify reducing or pausing
   an asserted workload while the world determines exact water access, rest,
   condition and outcome. They do not diagnose heat illness, establish
   dehydration/thirst, assign a threshold, or replace a body/time owner.
   [UConn Extension, *Heat Stress: Too Hot to Trot?*](https://extension.uconn.edu/publication/equine-heat-stress/)

9. **Startle/fear can overlap with other working-horse cues.** Horses are prey
   animals whose response to a perceived threat can be rapid flight; a stimulus
   unnoticed by a person may alarm a horse. Alertness, avoidance or sudden
   movement may be narrated in an independently established context, but do not
   prove what the horse perceived, an injury, pain, aggression, intent or a
   future action. [Rutgers NJAES, *The Basics of Equine Behavior*](https://njaes.rutgers.edu/FS525/)

## Boundaries for later use

- These are general premises, not a species catalogue, animal-state model,
  symptom-to-diagnosis map, welfare score, treatment protocol, dose, or
  historical-veterinary assertion.
- Exact animal identity, source, access, body state, water, load, rest,
  weather, time, action outcome and persistence remain existing world owners.
- A current animal, roost, horse condition, pain, fatigue, thirst, fear or
  encounter needs independent committed-world evidence; narration cannot create
  it from any premise above.
- Modern safety guidance only supports a cautious non-contact interaction
  boundary. It must not be rendered as anachronistic institution, equipment or
  mandatory procedure in 1230.
