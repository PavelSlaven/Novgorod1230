## Aphids, honeydew, ants, and distorted young growth

Sources: [Utah State University Extension, "Aphid Pests on Vegetables"](https://extension.usu.edu/planthealth/research/aphid-pests-on-vegetables); [Texas A&M AgriLife Extension, "Diagnosing Insect and Mite Damage"](https://landscapeipm.tamu.edu/ipm-for-ornamentals/diagnosing-insect-and-mite-damage-ornamentals/).

- Aphids feed on plant sap and congregate on succulent new growth, including
  young leaves and developing stems. Utah State lists bean as a potato-aphid
  host. Feeding can coincide with curled leaves and stunted, yellowed, or
  distorted plant structures. [Utah State Extension](https://extension.usu.edu/planthealth/research/aphid-pests-on-vegetables)
- Aphid feeding can leave sticky, shiny, sugar-rich honeydew. Ants consume it;
  Utah State says it attracts ants, which protect aphids from predators. Thus
  ants repeatedly moving over a colony or stems are compatible with
  honeydew-producing sap feeders, not proof of them. [Utah State Extension](https://extension.usu.edu/planthealth/research/aphid-pests-on-vegetables)
- Curling or distortion is nondiagnostic. Utah State notes that many aphid
  species cause similar damage and that virus symptoms can mimic other problems;
  tests are typically needed to confirm virus infection. Texas A&M likewise says
  deformity or irregular growth may have pest, disease, watering, fertilizer,
  or toxic causes, and identifies cause before pesticide use. [Utah State Extension](https://extension.usu.edu/planthealth/research/aphid-pests-on-vegetables) [Texas A&M AgriLife Extension](https://landscapeipm.tamu.edu/ipm-for-ornamentals/diagnosing-insect-and-mite-damage-ornamentals/)

**Gameplay/reconstruction boundary.** A player can observe clustered small
insects on young bean growth, curled or distorted leaves, sticky residue, and
ants as a connected field clue. The clue supports inspection and a tentative
description such as “consistent with sap-feeding insects”; it does not prove
aphids, species, hidden cause, disease status, severity, or a correct remedy.
Any response must use committed plant condition, visible organisms, season,
access, available method, and resulting state; do not turn a sign into a
diagnosis, treatment prescription, or deterministic recovery/failure.
