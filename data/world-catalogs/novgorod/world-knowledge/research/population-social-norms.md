# Candidate set: property, obligation, proof, authority, trade and measures

**Status:** unverified research candidates; not approved World Knowledge,
runtime law, a default action, an NPC status, or a fact about 1230 Novgorod.

## Reopened primary-text sources

- **PN-S1 — Extended *Russkaya Pravda***, [Moscow State University historical
  faculty edition](https://www.hist.msu.ru/ER/Etext/RP/prp_t.htm), the
  Trinity-list text, explicitly labelled second half of the fourteenth century
  by that edition. It preserves articles traditionally associated with earlier
  Rus legal material, but its manuscript date, redaction history, and
  non-Novgorodian generality prohibit treating it as an executable Novgorod
  1230 code.
- **PN-S2 — Smolensk–German treaty project**, [same institutional primary-text
  edition](https://www.hist.msu.ru/ER/Etext/RP/smolensk.htm), explicitly a
  mid-thirteenth-century project between Smolensk and German trading parties
  (Riga/Gotland). It is direct evidence only for its named treaty parties and
  places, never a rule for Novgorod.
- **PN-S3 — dating and provenance**, M. N. Tikhomirov, [*Lists and origins of
  the recensions of Russkaya Pravda*](https://www.hist.msu.ru/ER/Etext/RP/intro.htm),
  the scholarly introduction supplied with the MSU edition. It argues that the
  Extended Recension cannot predate 1125; sees it reflected in the
  thirteenth-century Smolensk treaty material; and advances an early-
  thirteenth-century Novgorod origin, associated with the 1209 uprising. The
  introduction records scholarly criticism of that reconstruction. It is a
  contested dating/provenance argument, not proof that every article was
  enacted Novgorod law in 1230.

## Source and integration limits

- S1’s displayed Trinity-list copy is fourteenth century. S3 advances, but
  also records criticism of, an early-thirteenth-century Novgorod provenance
  argument for the Extended Recension. S1 candidates are therefore only
  **inferred-medium 1200–1300 broad Rus / Novgorod compatibility**, never
  direct local proof that an article was operative law in 1230.
- S2 is more nearly contemporary to the target century, but it names Smolensk,
  Riga, Gotland and German parties. It has **zero** geographic transfer power
  to Novgorod without an independent Novgorod source.
- The candidates intentionally describe texts and bounded relations, not
  executable norms. They must not create a property title, possession, debt,
  entrusted item, tariff, witness, official, market, trade privilege, role,
  or authority.
- Existing local birch-bark evidence remains stronger for a Novgorod document
  category: debt record №73 and judicial record №510. It still does not supply
  general procedure, rates, measures, title, or office powers.
