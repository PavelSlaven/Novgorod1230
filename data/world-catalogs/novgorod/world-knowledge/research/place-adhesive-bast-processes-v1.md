# Adhesive and linden-bast processes v1

## Scope

Two bounded place04 premises only: aqueous collagen/gelatin animal-glue cooling and drying; water-soaking and separation/handling of already established linden bast. They add neither recipes nor present world state, availability, exact mechanics, temperatures, durations, tools, yields, or successful outcomes.

## Sources actually read

- OpenStax, [*Chemistry 2e: Colloids*](https://openstax.org/books/chemistry-2e/pages/11-5-colloids), “Gels”: aqueous gelatin sets as it cools. Technical textbook source; it supplies no adhesive recipe or bond result.
- Smithsonian Museum Conservation Institute, [*Painting Conservation Glossary of Terms*](https://mci.si.edu/painting-conservation-glossary-terms), “size”: identifies the relevant water-diluted animal-glue material as collagen/gelatin.
- Canadian Conservation Institute, [*The Salzinnes Antiphonal – Part 2*](https://www.canada.ca/en/conservation-institute/services/videos/salzinnes-treatment-textblock.html), “Tear repair”: documents gelatine adhesive in a repair drying between blotters. Modern conservation case, not historical bookbinding or woodworking instruction.
- U.S. Geological Survey, [*Plants of Louisiana: Tilia americana*](https://warcapps.usgs.gov/PlantID/Species/Details/163), “Plant Uses”: describes soaking named linden inner bark to separate tough fibres and subsequent fibre uses.
- USDA Forest Service, [*Tilia spp.: linden, basswood*](https://www.fs.usda.gov/nsl/Wpsm/Tilia.pdf), “Uses”: describes long, tough linden inner-bark bast fibres and cordage/mat/clothing uses.

## Authoring limits

First claim is an `inferred` composition: Smithsonian identifies collagen/gelatin animal glue, OpenStax establishes cooling gelation, and CCI gives a drying adhesive-work example. It deliberately excludes every-glue generalization, numerical conditions, application recipe, clamping, strength, and guarantee.

Second claim is a `medium`-confidence `editorial` reconstruction scoped to Novgorod Land 1200–1300. USDA’s genus-level material description and USGS’s named-species soaking account support a bounded transfer for already established linden bast. It establishes no linden in scene, no local bark resource, historical attestation, or transfer to nettle or other bast plants.
