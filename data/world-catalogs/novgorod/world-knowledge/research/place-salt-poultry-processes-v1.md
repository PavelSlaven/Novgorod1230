# Salt moisture and domestic-goose gathering: source basis

## Scope

Three candidate relations only: one about damp common salt and two about moving domestic geese as a group. They add neither a recipe nor a scene, animal, stock, container, weather value, command, or runtime behavior. The JSON fragment is not included by `production-v1/authoring.json` and has no verification binding; it remains for separate independent review.

## Existing coverage and gap

Existing salt claims cover salting food and salt in other material processes, not the bounded moisture-to-caking/dissolution relation for stored salt. Existing animal-care material covers care and health contexts, not group movement of domestic geese or the conditional effect of a handler's approach. These candidates do not add a care checklist, a whistle command, or an analogy from sheep.

## Candidate premises

- For common salt based on sodium chloride, moisture at particle contacts can produce local dissolution; subsequent drying can leave recrystallized bridges and caking. Composition and conditions affect this, so dampness does not predict caking for every salt mass. This is a qualitative mechanism, not an RH threshold, storage time, or prediction for one container.
- Modern small-flock guidance describes domestic geese as strongly flocking and capable of being herded from one area to another. This supports only possible group movement.
- Modern poultry handling guidance covering ducks and geese advises quiet, calm approach. It supports a conditional response relation, not obedience, a cue, or guaranteed control.

## Limits

No historical local husbandry custom, animal identity, flock size, present moisture, salt purity, temperature, humidity, container condition, or game state was researched or created. The two goose premises are modern husbandry transfers, not evidence that a player can always gather or direct geese.
