# Macro batch 19 process-gap closure — source rationale v1

## Scope

Seven B19 probes collapse into four reusable process families: bast and warp
control; flexible basket stock and clay selection; waterpower and pack-load
limits; and a contained response to burning cooking fat.  The candidate does
not create materials, tools, mills, animals, fires, current state, prices,
legal rules, craft success, injury, or any scenario outcome.

## Fibre and weaving

Musialak et al. describe retting as separation of bast fibres from the other
stem tissues through pectin cleavage.  The claim retains only the resulting
need to observe whether separation has progressed, not a recipe or duration.
The weaving reference identifies winding and spacing as causes of uneven warp
tension; the candidate makes that a qualitative diagnosis-before-adjustment
premise, rather than prescribing a loom or repair.

## Basketry and clay

Musgrove's basketry guidance states that dried willow absorbs water and becomes
flexible.  UAF identifies plasticity as essential for forming clay.  Together
they support material-condition checks while expressly leaving local stock,
composition, tools and results to the existing world owners.

## Water, loads, and cooking fat

DOE's hydropower overview gives the general dependency on water flow and head;
the candidate transfers it only to an already established water-driven system.
ICWE identifies added fatigue and injury risk from imbalanced pack loads, with
no numeric load limit.  NFPA's pan-fire guidance supplies the contained
turn-off/cover/no-water sequence only after an established pan of fat ignites.
