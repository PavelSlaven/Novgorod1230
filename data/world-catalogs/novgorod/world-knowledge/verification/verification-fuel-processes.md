# Independent verification: charcoal, light holders and pan handling

Root independently rendered and visually read B. A. Kolchin, MIA 32 (1953),
printed pages 40 and 166–168, from the IA RAS scan:
https://archaeolog.ru/media/series/mia/MIA_32.pdf . The researcher separately
identified the candidates; approval here is based on the actual page images.

K01–K07: APPROVE_WITH_LIMITS. Page 40 explicitly connects charcoal with
Ancient-Rus metallurgy. Pit production is the author's likely reconstruction,
partly explained through later technical analogy. Wood logs/branches are
covered by turf/clay; weak air access supports initial partial burning,
openings are then closed, wood carbonizes and the charcoal is allowed to cool.
Air regulation is a substantive process condition, not a guaranteed yield.
Do not import the numerical duration, yield or fuel ratios into exact mechanics.

Page 168, figure 146 and its preceding text: APPROVE_WITH_LIMITS for iron
splinter/candle holder forms. The author explicitly infers iron construction
for these unexamined utensils; it is not a metallographic test of every holder,
nor a dated Novgorod 1230 light source. No wax/oil/fuel, lit object or successful
lighting follows from holder compatibility.

Page 166: APPROVE_WITH_LIMITS for the chapelnik/pan lifter used to insert and
remove iron frying pans from an oven, and its socketed/tanged handle forms.
These are broad historical tool/function premises, not a ready oven or pan.

Historical compatibility remains inferred/medium and domain-internal. Existing
typed input/output/use predicates are reused; air-control premises use the
existing contextual supported_fact predicate. No new process owner, recipe,
source quantity or actor skill is introduced.
