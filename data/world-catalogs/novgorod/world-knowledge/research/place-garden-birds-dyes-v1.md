# Garden pests, rock pigeons, and leaf dyes: candidate source basis

## Scope

Five candidate premises only: two bounded garden-snail-and-slug relations, one rock-pigeon relation, one leaf-to-dye-source relation, and one emollient/occlusive-skin relation. They add no present animal, plant, food, building, nest, recipe, tool, dye colour, weather value, or runtime behaviour. The JSON fragment is not in `production-v1/authoring.json`; it remains a candidate pending independent source/domain review.

## Candidate premises

- Garden snails and slugs can favour moist sheltered conditions and are most active at night and on cloudy or foggy days. This is not a claim about all land snails or gastropods, local abundance, or a given garden.
- Garden snails and slugs can feed on cabbage leaves and their scraping can leave irregular holes. Damage signs do not identify a particular culprit.
- Rock pigeons can use sheltered, flat built sites and can congregate in flocks around abundant food resources. This is modern ecology, not a claim of birds, nests, grain, bread, or a historical local population.
- Plant leaves can be source material for a dye bath, with result depending on plant and process. It is not a recipe or an availability claim; UVM gives sorrel foliage as a modern example.
- Where a preparation is actually emollient or occlusive, it can form a film that traps moisture and supports hydration of dry skin. This does not transfer the property to every fat or oil, establish medical treatment or historical medicine, or apply to burns, weeping, or infected skin.

## Sources and actual access

- UC Statewide IPM Program, [*Snails and Slugs*](https://ipm.ucanr.edu/home-and-landscape/snails-and-slugs/), accessed through official-page web fallback. The local `browser-harness --doctor` showed no live daemon, so Chrome/CDP was unavailable. Read “Identification and Biology”, “Damage”, and “Cultural Control”. They cover both snails and slugs: night/cloudy/foggy activity, moisture/shelter context, irregular holes, and cabbage among seriously damaged plants.
- Iowa State University Natural Resources Extension, [*Nuisance Birds: Damage Management*](https://naturalresources.extension.iastate.edu/encyclopedia/nuisance-birds-damage-management), accessed through official-page web fallback. Read “Rock Pigeons”: sheltered flat built nesting sites, single/flock movement for roosting/feeding/flying, and greater congregation around abundant food. Its geographic examples are modern Iowa and do not establish Novgorod conditions.
- University of Vermont Extension, [*Making Dyes from Your Garden*](https://www.uvm.edu/extension/news/making-dyes-your-garden), accessed through official-page web fallback. Read dye-source and process passages. It says roots, leaves, and flowers can be dye sources and names sorrel and spinach foliage as producing a dark-green dye bath. It does not demonstrate local medieval availability or a general dye recipe.
- NHS, [*Emollients*](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/emollients/), accessed through official-page web fallback. Read overview and product-type passages. It says emollients cover skin with a protective film that traps moisture; greasy ointments are very moisturising for very dry, thickened skin. It does not establish that ordinary animal fat or oil has the required emollient/occlusive property, nor any treatment, dose, or use on burns, infected, or weeping skin.
