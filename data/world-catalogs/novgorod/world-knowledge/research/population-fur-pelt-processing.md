# Fur-bearing pelt preparation and preservation — bounded research

**Status:** candidate research only. This note neither approves claims nor
creates a pelt, a hunted animal, a furrier, a cooling place, water, tools,
skill, stock, or a processing facility in any scene.

## Boundary with existing historical context

The active corpus already records that fur reached Novgorod from northern
districts in the XI century (Makarov, ed., *Archaeology of the Northern Russian
Village X–XIII Centuries*, vol. 3, 2009, printed p. 69; source/evidence
`population-processes-fur-exchange`). That is an exchange envelope, not a
claim that a particular pelt, animal, worker, or workshop exists in 1230.

The active CCI-derived claims already cover rawhide wetting/drying and distinct
tannages. They are not repeated. The candidates below are narrower universal
relations about preserving hair-bearing skins after flaying; they do **not**
prove an Old Rus dressing recipe or local availability. Historical compatibility
can only be composed later with separately approved fur/leather context and
committed scene inputs.

## Source fully read

Carole Dignard and Janet Mason, [*Caring for leather, skin and fur*](https://www.canada.ca/en/conservation-institute/services/preventive-conservation/guidelines-collections/caring-leather-skin-fur.html), Canadian Conservation Institute, 2018, was opened and read in full from the publisher page. It is a preventive-conservation technical reference, therefore direct for the described qualitative material/process relations, but not an archaeological demonstration of a thirteenth-century Novgorod workshop.

Relevant passages:

- **“Skin structure and processing”**: processing begins after skin is removed from a flayed animal; it cleanses the skin and prevents putrefaction. It lists washing/soaking, scraping and mechanical actions as typical steps for skin processing, but does not prescribe one sequence for every skin.
- **“Furs”**: fur-skins may be processed through different routes; factors critical for retaining hair include avoiding physical damage during scraping/manipulation, quick cooling, and prompt thorough cleansing to prevent bacterial growth that risks loosening hair.
- **“Rawhide”**: mechanical defleshing and frame-drying describes the *rawhide* route specifically. It is deliberately not generalized here to all hair-bearing pelt processing, because that section also says rawhide is unhaired.

## Exclusions

- No historical Novgorod tanning/dressing method, agent, vessel, season, facility,
  profession, stock, species, pelt quality, ownership, or market supply is
  asserted.
- No fixed cooling temperature, deadline, drying method, flesh-removal
  requirement, water resistance, health claim, or guaranteed hair retention
  follows from this source.
- Rawhide defleshing/drying remains a distinct, already-covered route; it is
  not evidence that every fur-bearing pelt is defleshed, dried, rawhide, or
  dressed alike.
- Exact resource use, time, heat, access, contamination, decay, and resulting
  object state remain world-state and mechanics responsibilities.

## Research result

Three new universal, qualified material/process candidates; zero new historical
availability claims. All need independent verification before any authoring.
