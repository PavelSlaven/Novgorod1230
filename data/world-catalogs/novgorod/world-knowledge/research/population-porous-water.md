# Porous-material water behaviour research

**Status:** research only; no production authoring, approval, or special
material handler.

## Existing-claim boundary

The current pack already states that soil texture affects infiltration
(`claim:soil-texture-affects-infiltration-rate`), that wood exchanges moisture
with air, and that sliding friction depends on the actual contacting materials
and load. These candidates concern general interfacial and connected-pore
mechanisms; they do not provide a water-friction coefficient or duplicate a
soil-specific infiltration rate.

## Sources read

1. OpenStax, [*College Physics 2e*, 11.8 “Cohesion and Adhesion in Liquids:
Surface Tension and Capillary Action”](https://openstax.org/books/college-physics-2e/pages/11-8-cohesion-and-adhesion-in-liquids-surface-tension-and-capillary-action).
   The full section was read. It defines contact angle, distinguishes cohesive
   and adhesive forces, and gives the capillary-rise conditions.
2. U.S. Geological Survey, [*Water Science Glossary*](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/water-science-glossary), entries **percolation**, **permeability**, and **porosity** (15 June 2018). The full relevant entries were read. They distinguish pore volume from the size and interconnection of openings required for liquid movement.

## Candidate premises

| ID | Atomic premise | Exact anchor | Limits required in any future use |
|---|---|---|---|
| PW-01 | Whether a liquid spreads or beads on a surface depends on the relative cohesive liquid forces and adhesive liquid-surface forces; contact angle expresses that relation. | OpenStax 11.8, “Adhesion and Capillary Action” and “Contact Angle” | A wettable surface is not thereby porous or permeable. No particular material, contact angle, wetted area, or friction result follows. |
| PW-02 | In a narrow tube, capillary action can raise or suppress a liquid according to the liquid-solid combination and contact angle. | OpenStax 11.8, “Capillary Action”; discussion of Fig. 11.31 | This is a capillary geometry premise, not proof of a capillary path in a solid, a rate, or a source of liquid. |
| PW-03 | For a specified capillary, rise height varies with surface tension, contact angle, tube radius, liquid density, and gravity; smaller radius can raise a liquid higher. | OpenStax 11.8, equation 11.51 and following discussion | No numerical height, direction, or movement in an unspecified pore network may be inferred. |
| PW-04 | Percolation is water movement through openings in rock or soil. | USGS glossary, “percolation” | It requires actual openings and water; it does not establish an opening path, infiltration rate, or an accessible underground source at a scene. |
| PW-05 | Permeability is the capacity of a material to transmit liquid/water; transport depends on pore-space size and how well pores are interconnected. | USGS glossary, “permeability” and “porosity” | Total porosity alone is insufficient. Closed or isolated pores do not establish through-flow; no object-specific permeability or volume follows. |
| PW-06 | Porosity measures void space, while open/interconnected and closed/isolated pores differ in whether water can move between spaces. | USGS glossary, “porosity” | Do not equate visible roughness, absorbency, wetting, or any porosity with a connected flow path. This is a distinction, not a material inventory. |

## Non-inferences

Neither source supplies a universal wet-friction rule. Wetting, capillary uptake,
porosity, permeability, and bulk water flow have separate stated conditions.
They also do not establish a specific material's absorbency, water quantity,
damage, drying time, or scene presence.
