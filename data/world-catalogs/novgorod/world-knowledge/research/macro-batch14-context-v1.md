# Macro batch 14 context — editorial rationale v1

## Scope

This note supplies three deliberately narrow, game-useful reconstructions for
static batch-14 gaps.  They are not findings about a particular archaeological
site, merchant's stock, household, or legal rule.

## Reading a mixed shoreline trace

Existing World Knowledge already treats driven stakes as a documented building
technique and charcoal as a material/process context.  A mixed observation of
stakes, charcoal and uneven ground is therefore compatible with earlier human
use, but its function is underdetermined: it can be a former structure,
working spot, fire place, bank work, or redeposited material.  The useful
ordinary inference is to inspect it as a possible prior use of the place while
choosing a camp from present ground, water and access.  It cannot identify a
dwelling, date, owner, extent, safety, or entitlement.

## Weighing at a market

E. A. Rybina, *Торговля средневекового Новгорода: историко-археологические
очерки* (2001), is registered in the project import inventory as evidence for
Novgorod trade and weighing practice, but has no page-level extraction here.
The claim below is consequently an editorial reconstruction, not a statement
of a named Novgorod unit or rule: a sale by weight needs a mutually observable
comparison, and an apparent discrepancy can be checked by repeating that
comparison.  It creates neither a standard unit, price, official inspector,
legal procedure, nor result of the dispute.

## A stranger at the door

The existing ordinary-lifeways premise already treats hospitality as bounded by
resources, trust and risk.  For a road household in rain, the smallest usable
extension is that temporary aid is a conditional social practice: the host can
ask, assess immediate risk and capacity, offer a limited dry or warm place, or
refuse/redirect.  It is an editorial reconstruction allowed by WK contract
§0.2, not a claimed mandatory medieval custom.  It establishes no right of
entry, meal, bed, trust, payment, gender rule, or uniform reaction.
