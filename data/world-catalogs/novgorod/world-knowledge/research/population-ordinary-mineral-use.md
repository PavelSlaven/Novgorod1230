# Ordinary mineral use — bounded historical research

**Status:** candidate research only.  No production, scene stock, exposed
stone, sand, soil, hearth, source right, tool, worker or finished edge follows
from this file.

## Existing-corpus boundary

I checked the active terrain/material, construction and physical-interaction
fragments.  They already cover a qualified regional Quaternary background,
monumental quarry/plaster contexts, pottery temper, stone weight, and the
conditional physical possibility of abrasive hard-stone work.  This pass does
not repeat those claims or treat modern geology as medieval extraction.

The independently inspectable non-monumental use found is a different layer:
the historical Ancient-Rus archaeological corpus records natural stone as the
working material of whetstones and grinding wheels in the maintenance of iron
edges.  It does **not** turn any scene stone into such an implement.

## Source read

B. A. Kolchin, [*Chernaia metallurgiia i metalloobrabotka v drevnei Rusi
(domongol'skii period)*](https://archaeolog.ru/media/series/mia/MIA_32.pdf),
Moscow: Academy of Sciences USSR, 1953, *Materialy i issledovaniia po
arkheologii SSSR* 32.  The official IA RAS scan is locally available as
`.tmp-pr92-sources/mia-32.pdf`; its printed pp. 109–110 (chapter “Tekhnika
metalloobrabotki”) were read from the local text extract.  The passage says
that grinding circles and bars were made from natural stone, naming sandstone,
emery and corundum among archaeological materials; it also says small
whetstone bars frequently found in burials and cultural layers could serve
only to sharpen dulled blades of knives, shears, scythes and similar tools.
The book’s corpus is pre-Mongol Rus broadly, not a dated Novgorod household.

## Negative result and boundary

No independently opened source in this pass supported a dated Novgorod/XIII
ordinary hearth-stone, sand-levelling or domestic soil-use relation without
sliding into either monumental construction, ceramic temper, road/landing
surfaces or present-scene stock.  Those examples are therefore not promoted.

The two candidates above are deliberately narrow: they may compose with a
known stone implement and a real edge-maintenance attempt, but they never
license an arbitrary found stone as a whetstone or materialize any mineral
resource.
