## Appendix B — independent LIFE3 CDC/NIOSH review

**Scope:** LIFE3 only; this is not a self-review of LIFE2 or a production
approval.  I independently opened both official pages and read the named
sections/table.  Each retained relation is a bounded public-health framework,
not evidence of any actor's exposure, infection, wound, immunity, medication,
diagnosis, severity or outcome.

| ID | Verdict | Checked source and exact admissible form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| LIFE3-01 | APPROVE_WITH_LIMITS | CDC/NIOSH, *Chain of Infection Components*, last reviewed 2022, enumerates microorganism, reservoir/source, portal of exit, mode of transport, portal of entry and susceptible host.  Use: “The CDC/NIOSH chain-of-infection framework describes source/reservoir, exit, transport, entry and a susceptible host as transmission components.”  It does not establish a complete chain or transmission in a scene. |
| LIFE3-02 | REWRITE | The same NIOSH page lists airborne, droplet, contact, bites and sharps among its direct modes, and contaminated equipment, vectors, food and water among indirect modes; the separate CDC air guideline classifies droplet nuclei differently.  Preserve the source scope: “The CDC/NIOSH training framework lists contact, droplets, bites, contaminated equipment, vectors, food and water among possible pathogen transport modes.”  Do not universalize its direct/indirect taxonomy or infer contamination. |
| LIFE3-03 | APPROVE_WITH_LIMITS | NIOSH defines a portal of entry as an opening where a pathogen may enter and gives body openings, incisions and wounds.  Use: “Possible portals of entry include body openings and breaks in the skin barrier.”  This is neither evidence of exposure/infection nor a statement that every opening transmits infection. |
| LIFE3-04 | APPROVE_WITH_LIMITS | CDC, *C. Air: Guidelines for Environmental Infection Control in Health-Care Facilities* (2003 content, page dated 2023), Table 3 for tuberculosis, lists airborne droplet-nuclei concentration and exposure duration under factors affecting severity/outcomes.  Keep exactly tuberculosis/airborne-droplet-nuclei scoped; no universal dose-duration equation, threshold or individual prediction. |
| LIFE3-05 | APPROVE_WITH_LIMITS | NIOSH's “Susceptible Host” section lists age, health, comorbidities, immune system, nutrition, infective dose and medications as factors affecting susceptibility.  Use: “Host susceptibility can be influenced by multiple factors, including those listed in the CDC/NIOSH framework.”  It cannot infer any named person's susceptibility, immune status, medication or outcome. |

**Outcome:** all five sources/candidate IDs were checked.  LIFE3-02 requires
the taxonomy-safe rewrite; LIFE3-01 and LIFE3-03--05 are eligible only with
their stated source-specific conditions.  No treatment or actor-level health
