# Winter fodder and animal feeding — targeted negative research

**Status:** research only. No production/matrix change and no self-approval.
This pass separates a date-compatible Novgorod resource context from universal
equine biology. It does **not** claim a locally attested feeding routine,
winter stock, ration, stable, owner, or present animal.

## Primary corpus opened

The HSE / Institute for Slavic Studies RAS *Old Russian Birchbark Letters*
records below were opened in full, including dating, editorial contents,
manuscript text and collapsed Russian translations where supplied.

1. [Letter no. 986](https://gramoty.ru/birchbark/document/show/novgorod/986/):
   Novgorod; stratigraphy 1160s–mid-1190s, editorial external date last
   quarter XII–first quarter XIII; fragmentary household account.
2. [Letter no. 616](https://gramoty.ru/birchbark/document/show/novgorod/616/):
   Novgorod; 1210s–1240s; transport/payment fragment. Rechecked only to
   exclude its unrelated boat evidence from this topic.
3. [Letter no. 147](https://gramoty.ru/birchbark/document/show/novgorod/147/):
   Novgorod; 1220–1240 / stratigraphic 1220s–1230s; opened after a search hit
   for *пожня*. Its actual text concerns a *почестье*, not a hay meadow.
4. [Letter no. 358](https://gramoty.ru/birchbark/document/show/novgorod/358/):
   Novgorod; XIV century (stratigraphic 1340s–1360s). It is the genuine direct
   order to feed horses good hay and oats, but is deliberately excluded as too
   late for a XII–XIII compatibility premise.
5. [Letter no. 53](https://gramoty.ru/birchbark/document/show/novgorod/53/):
   XIV century; direct mowing/hay-dispute text, likewise excluded as too late.
6. [Letter no. 275/266](https://gramoty.ru/birchbark/document/show/novgorod/275/266/):
   XIV century; direct daily oat-feeding instruction for a named horse,
   likewise excluded as too late.

## Candidate causal premises

| ID | Atomic premise (RU / EN) | Exact source finding | Directness and strict limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| FP-01 | **Сено → упомянуто в → хозяйственной расчётной записи. / Hay → is mentioned in → a household account.** | Letter 986's official Russian translation: «[за] … две куны; за сено две резаны; а за масло … две резаны». | FP-02 | **Сено и пастбищный фураж → признаны кормом для → здоровых лошадей. / Hay and pasture forage → are recognised feeds for → healthy horses.** | [Merck Veterinary Manual, “Feeding Practices in Horses and Other Equids”](https://www.merckvetmanual.com/management-and-nutrition/nutrition-horses/feeding-practices-in-horses-and-other-equids), opening feeding-practices statement: “Most healthy horses should be given free access to hay and/or pasture forages …”. | **Direct/high** for the universal veterinary feed relation; historical Novgorod applicability is only a biological compatibility premise. | FP-03 | **Дата-совместимое сено + отдельно подтверждённый конский контекст + универсальное отношение «лошадь—фураж» → допускают → причинно возможное кормление лошади сеном. / Date-compatible hay + independently evidenced horse context + universal horse–forage relation → permit → causally possible feeding of a horse with hay.** | Composition only: FP-01 supplies the local dated material context; FP-02 supplies the biological relation. ## Excluded direct operational texts

The following remain useful negative temporal controls, not candidate evidence:

| Horse → feed → hay/oats | Letter 358: «коней корми добрым сеном» and «кони корми овсом». | Direct but XIV century (stratigraphic 1340s–1360s), so it cannot substantiate c.1230 custom. |

## Strict conclusion

FP-01 through FP-03 provide a narrow compositional basis: historically
compatible hay as a material context and a universal horse–forage feed
relation. They do not close the separate historical-practice question of
winter storage, deliberate provisioning, forage transfer, herd management or
any exact diet. Those need a dated source which explicitly says so.

Next evidence path: independently opened XII–XIII Novgorod-land estate,
tax/landholding, agricultural-archaeology or textual source that explicitly
links hay/oats/fodder to animal care, haymaking or storage with a date anchor.
