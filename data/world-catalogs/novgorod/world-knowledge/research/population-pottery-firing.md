# Pottery firing — bounded historical and causal research

**Status:** research candidates only.  No production, approval, kiln, potter,
fuel, clay source, firing temperature, firing duration, vessel, workshop,
stock, access right or successful result is created by this file.

## Existing-corpus boundary

The active corpus already has clay plasticity; drying shrinkage and its
cracking risk; steam damage from rapid heating with pore water; the
quartz-transition risk; and firing/body effects on porosity.  It also has
bounded medieval Novgorod-region facts for suitable local clay, temper and
wheel-made pottery.  These are not repeated below.

The missing causal fact is narrower: firing can transform a dried clay body
into a ceramic body through thermal change.  That is universal physical
reality, not historical availability.  The historical source below is a
broad Old Rus firing-technology envelope, not a claim that a kiln existed in
a particular Novgorod scene in 1230.

## Sources independently opened

1. T. Neil Davis, [“Stoneware Pottery”](https://www.gi.alaska.edu/index.php/alaska-science-forum/stoneware-pottery-0), *Alaska Science Forum*,
   Geophysical Institute, University of Alaska Fairbanks (7 December 1981).
   The full article was read.  The paragraphs beginning “Further drying
   occurs...” and “After some hours of firing...” describe loss of chemically
   bound water and vitrification: chalklike clay fuses to a dense glassy body
   whose matrix is bound by growing aluminium-silicate crystals.  It is a
   scientific material source, not historical evidence for Rus'.
2. [“Goncharnoe remeslo drevnei Rusi. Tipy goncharnykh gornov”](https://bibliotekar.ru/2-8-82-drevnyaya-rus/67.htm), accessible electronic reprint under the collection heading
   “Feudal city of Ancient Rus' in the 10th–12th centuries: craft, trade.”
   The full page was read.  It explicitly states that urban ceramic products
   were fired in special kilns rather than ordinary domestic ovens and
   describes a two-tier kiln: fuel burned in the lower chamber; hot gases
   passed through openings in the floor to the upper chamber containing the
   pieces.  **The landing page supplies no named scholarly author or edition.**
   It is retained as a concrete, inspectable historical lead for independent
   bibliographic/source validation, not as self-approved archaeological proof.

## Result and verifier priority

PFR-01 is the new causal prerequisite needed to bridge existing dried-clay
facts and ceramic-body transformation; its historic availability must be
supplied separately by existing Novgorod clay/temper/wheel context or a
future dated firing source.  PFR-02–03 are useful historical leads but must
first be traced to their scholarly edition or replaced by a primary
excavation/publication before any normalization.  Do not silently promote
their reprint wording merely because the underlying statement is plausible.

No evidence found in this pass establishes an ordinary firing installation
in Novgorod in the year 1230.  That absence does not negate the broader
historical compatibility envelope; it prohibits materializing a kiln, fuel,
craftsman or output from this research alone.
