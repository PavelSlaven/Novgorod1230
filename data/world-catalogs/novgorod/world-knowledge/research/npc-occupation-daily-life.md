# Novgorod c.1230: NPC, occupation, daily life — research candidates

**Status:** research-only candidate corpus. It does not create an NPC, household, workshop, social rank, job, possession, schedule, or scene presence.

## Sources

- **S1:** [Novgorod birch-bark letter no. 73](https://gramoty.ru/birchbark/document/show/novgorod/73/) — primary document; database metadata dates it 1220–1240, stratigraphically the 1220s–1230s, and classifies it as a debt record.
- **S2:** [Novgorod birch-bark letter no. 510](https://gramoty.ru/birchbark/document/show/novgorod/510/) — primary document; database metadata dates it 1220–1240 and classifies it as a judicial record with accusation.
- **S3:** [Novgorod birch-bark letter no. 335](https://gramoty.ru/birchbark/document/show/novgorod/335/) — primary document; 1160–1180, a private letter whose catalogue summary identifies an order for gold kolts and a helmet. It is temporal background, not direct 1230 evidence.
- **S4:** [Brisbane, Makarov & Nosov (eds.), *The Archaeology of Medieval Novgorod in Context*](https://doi.org/10.2307/j.ctvh1dqcg) — academic regional synthesis of the city and hinterland.
- **S5:** [Volgina (2024), “Agricultural implements of medieval Novgorod”](https://doi.org/10.24852/pa2024.2.48) — archaeological study of the implement assemblage and chronology.
- **S6:** [Hamilton-Dyer, Brisbane & Maltby (2017), “Fish, feather, fur and forest”](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.quaint.2016.04.024) — zooarchaeological study of wild-animal exploitation in medieval Novgorod and its territory.
- **S7:** [Maltby & Brisbane (eds., 2020), *Animals and Archaeology in Northern Medieval Russia*](https://doi.org/10.2307/j.ctv138wsxr) — regional zooarchaeological synthesis.

## Compositional factual candidates

### NPC-01 — debt records are directly attested everyday documentary practice

- **Period / region / location envelope:** 1220–1240; Novgorod city, Nerevsky excavation, estate B.
- **Documented practice:** letter no. 73 is a complete business record classified as a debt list/record, with stratigraphic dating to the 1220s–1230s ([S1](https://gramoty.ru/birchbark/document/show/novgorod/73/)).
- **Plausible inference:** people in the documentary setting could need to remember, communicate, or contest obligations; this does not establish a professional scribe, merchant, creditor, or debtor for an arbitrary NPC.
- **Confidence:** **high** for this document and its metadata; **low** for general literacy rate or occupation distribution.
- **Exclusions / conflicts:** no universal literacy claim; no assumed coin, amount, interest rule, enforceable contract, or named relationship beyond the document’s own content.
- **Gameplay use:** a contextual fact for a source-grounded social/economic query after an actual record or obligation exists; never a generator for NPC debts.

### NPC-02 — accusation and judicial communication are directly attested, not a universal procedure

- **Period / region / location envelope:** 1220–1240; Novgorod city.
- **Documented practice:** the primary-source catalogue identifies letter no. 510 as a judicial record with accusation ([S2](https://gramoty.ru/birchbark/document/show/novgorod/510/)).
- **Plausible inference:** disputes could involve written communication in at least some Novgorod settings.
- **Confidence:** **high** for the classified document; **low** for the exact court, officeholder, sanction, procedure, or access of a particular person.
- **Exclusions / conflicts:** do not create police, court staff, literacy, a lawsuit, guilt, or a legal norm from the candidate. A complaint is not proof of the alleged fact.
- **Gameplay use:** distinguish historically attested documentary dispute from a free-form NPC allegation; existing social/legal owners must resolve any actual consequence.

### NPC-03 — commissioned specialist work is attested before, not proven at, 1230

- **Period / region / location envelope:** 1160–1180, Novgorod city; historical background only for c.1230.
- **Documented practice:** letter no. 335 is catalogued as a private letter containing an order for four gold kolts and a helmet ([S3](https://gramoty.ru/birchbark/document/show/novgorod/335/)).
- **Plausible inference:** commissioned skilled manufacture and negotiated material/value terms existed in medieval Novgorod.
- **Confidence:** **high** for the specific earlier document; **medium-low** for continuity to 1230; **low** for a particular workshop or craftsperson.
- **Exclusions / conflicts:** no default goldsmith, armourer, helmet, precious metal stock, price, guild, or workshop in a 1230 scene. The letter does not prove mass production or a standard occupational hierarchy.
- **Gameplay use:** support a qualified historical-compatibility response when a causally established commission/workshop is already in play; it is not a recipe or inventory source.

### NPC-04 — agricultural labour is a regional practice, not every NPC’s job

- **Period / region / location envelope:** medieval Novgorod and its agrarian hinterland; established field, yard, store, or farming household.
- **Documented practice:** the archaeological agricultural-implement assemblage has been systematically studied and dated ([S5](https://doi.org/10.24852/pa2024.2.48)).
- **Plausible inference:** agricultural work is historically compatible for a rural household or settlement context with a real source and season.
- **Confidence:** **medium-high** for regional practice; **low** for an individual’s occupation, landholding, tool access, crop, or current task.
- **Exclusions / conflicts:** no peasant NPC, plough, harvest, corvée obligation, field ownership, or yield appears merely because the scene is rural.
- **Gameplay use:** contextual grounding for an existing rural activity/process; time, weather, property, and material owners determine actual labour and outputs.

### NPC-05 — animal keeping and animal use are managed relations

- **Period / region / location envelope:** medieval Novgorod and its region; an established household, byre, market, transport, or refuse context.
- **Documented practice:** S7 is a regional zooarchaeological synthesis dedicated to human–animal relations in medieval Novgorod and its region ([S7](https://doi.org/10.2307/j.ctv138wsxr)).
- **Plausible inference:** animal care, slaughter, transport, or exchange can be historically compatible where a specific domesticated animal and its controller/source already exist.
- **Confidence:** **medium** for regional managed-animal context; **low** for a named person’s profession, wealth, animal ownership, or animal health.
- **Exclusions / conflicts:** no herd, horse, dairy, cart, butcher, stable, or veterinary practice; a faunal remain does not prove a live animal in a present scene.
- **Gameplay use:** explains why a grounded household/market/transport context may query animal-related practices without granting presence or mechanics.

### NPC-06 — fishing, hunting, and fur work must not collapse into one “woodsman” role

- **Period / region / location envelope:** medieval Novgorod and its territory; an established freshwater, forest, wetland, hunting, or trade context.
- **Documented practice:** S6 studies distinct exploitation of fish, birds, wild mammals, fur, and forest resources across the region ([S6](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.quaint.2016.04.024)).
- **Plausible inference:** different resource practices could produce overlapping but non-identical skills, tools, seasons, routes, and trade connections.
- **Confidence:** **medium** for regional exploitation; **low** for a single individual, a catch, a pelt, a hunting right, or a market destination.
- **Exclusions / conflicts:** no generic hunter/trapper/fisher NPC, no prey, no fur stock, and no automatic success. Food-bone frequency and fur-trade volume are not interchangeable evidence.
- **Gameplay use:** keeps factual grounding compositional: query the established setting and source separately instead of assigning a stock occupation.

### NPC-07 — a household is a contextual unit, not an inferred nuclear family

- **Period / region / location envelope:** medieval Novgorod city and hinterland; only a canonically or causally established dwelling/property.
- **Documented practice:** S4 is a centre/periphery archaeological synthesis that treats buildings, properties, domestic economy, and settlement context as archaeological subjects ([S4](https://doi.org/10.2307/j.ctvh1dqcg)).
- **Plausible inference:** a dwelling can be a useful scene-level context for storage, labour, food preparation, visitors, dependants, and animals when these are independently grounded.
- **Confidence:** **medium** for household/property as an archaeological analytical context; **low** for kinship, co-residence, gendered tasks, wealth, privacy, or daily schedule.
- **Exclusions / conflicts:** no spouse, child, servant, workshop, domestic animal, or possessions from the word “household”; do not map modern nuclear-family assumptions onto a medieval property.
- **Gameplay use:** establishes a neutral location envelope for other evidence-backed candidates, without materializing inhabitants.

### NPC-08 — repetitive routines are plausible inference, not a recovered timetable

- **Period / region / location envelope:** any actually grounded medieval Novgorod or north-west Rus’ scene around 1230.
- **Documented practice:** sources above demonstrate particular records, material practices, and regional resource use; they do **not** provide a complete day-by-day schedule for an ordinary named individual around 1230 ([S1](https://gramoty.ru/birchbark/document/show/novgorod/73/), [S4](https://doi.org/10.2307/j.ctvh1dqcg), [S6](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.quaint.2016.04.024)).
- **Plausible inference:** people whose actual roles and material circumstances are known may have recurring maintenance, travel, work, eating, and rest needs.
- **Confidence:** **high** for the evidential limit; **low** for any proposed NPC daily routine.
- **Exclusions / conflicts:** no fixed waking hour, workday, meal count, religious attendance, gender role, or occupational schedule; do not convert contemporary routine into a historical fact.
- **Gameplay use:** requires the time/activity and NPC owners to derive a committed schedule from established activity, needs, weather, access, and prior state.

### NPC-09 — social labels need source-specific evidence

- **Period / region / location envelope:** Novgorod and north-west Rus’, c.1230.
- **Documented practice:** the source set supports particular documentary, craft, agricultural, and resource contexts, not a closed class system or population-wide occupational taxonomy ([S1](https://gramoty.ru/birchbark/document/show/novgorod/73/), [S3](https://gramoty.ru/birchbark/document/show/novgorod/335/), [S5](https://doi.org/10.24852/pa2024.2.48)).
- **Plausible inference:** a person’s visible task, equipment, access, and social connections may provide limited in-world evidence about current activity, not a canonical identity.
- **Confidence:** **high** for the limitation; **low** for any individual status inference.
- **Exclusions / conflicts:** no generic “merchant”, “peasant”, “craftsman”, “slave”, “boyar”, “soldier”, or “priest” created from clothing, location, player wording, or a single tool.
- **Gameplay use:** demand an existing identity/role source before an NPC is named or given a status; otherwise return only bounded contextual knowledge or unresolved.

## Cross-cutting authoring constraints

- A document proves the document’s specified setting and statement, not the truth of every allegation inside it.
- Archaeological assemblages establish regional/material context; they do not identify a present individual, ownership, motive, or schedule.
- “Historically compatible” is weaker than “common”, and both are weaker than “present here now”.
- Do not use modern job titles, guild structures, industrial specialization, compulsory schooling, modern working hours, or a nuclear-family default as 1230 facts without a narrower source.
