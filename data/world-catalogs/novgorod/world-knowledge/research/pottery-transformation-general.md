# General clay-to-ceramic transformation — research candidates

**Status:** research only; no self-approval or production authoring.  This
pass deliberately replaces neither a historical firing context nor the
previous stoneware-specific vitrification candidate.  It addresses the
narrow universal distinction between dried, re-plasticizable clay and a clay
body that has undergone ceramic firing.

## Source independently opened

- Aalto University OpenLearning, [*Ceramic Handbook: Ceramic
  Materials*](https://openlearning.aalto.fi/course/section.php?id=2034),
  “Ceramics” section, page labelled “Ceramic materials Aalto 2021,” opened in
  full on 2026-09-04.  The section says that clay fired at high temperature
  hardens and condenses into durable ceramic; dried clay can repeatedly be
  returned to a plastic form with water, whereas clay heated past the ceramic
  change becomes hard, no longer dissolves in water, and does not return to
  its plastic form.  It explains the change as heat-altered chemical crystal
  structure.  The source is university teaching material under CC BY 4.0;
  it is a universal material-science source, not historical evidence for a
  Rus' kiln, craftsperson, fuel, vessel, or firing event.

## Composition boundary

PTG-01 and PTG-03 provide the missing state contrast: wet/dried clay and
ceramically fired clay must not be treated as interchangeable after water is
added.  PTG-02 supplies only the qualitative transformation basis.  Any
historical Novgorod-compatible craft use still requires separately grounded
clay, appropriate heat/process, place, time, and actor context; these
candidates create none of them.
