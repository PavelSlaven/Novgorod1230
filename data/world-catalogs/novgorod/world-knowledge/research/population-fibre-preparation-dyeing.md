# Fibre preparation and dyeing — northern Rus / Novgorod envelope

**Status:** research candidates only; no self-approval and no production
change.  The point of this pass is a causal substrate between a real bast-plant
stem and spin-ready fibre.  It does not materialise a crop, tool, workshop,
worker, yarn, cloth, dye-bath or garment.

## Independently opened primary archaeological publication

1. A. V. Kudryashov, [«Деревянные орудия и приспособления для обработки
   прядильных растений с поселения Луковец (X–XII вв.)»](https://bibliotekar.ru/rusNovgorod/164.htm),
   *Новгородский музейный вестник* 21 (2007), full article. The article
   describes excavated wooden tools from Lukovets on the Sheksna, a northern-Rus
   settlement whose excavated building horizons are dendrochronologically and
   radiocarbon dated from the mid-10th to late 12th century (one excavation to
   the beginning of the 13th).  The relevant passages are headed **«Среди
   изделий из дерева…»**, **«Великолепной находкой…»**, **«Трепала…»**,
   **«Чесала…»**, and **«Присутствие прядения…»**.  They identify the tools
   and their functions, rather than merely listing a textile type.

The source is not a Novgorod-1230 workshop inventory.  It is direct evidence
for an earlier northern-Rus technical sequence; compatibility with the
1100–1300 Novgorod-Land envelope is therefore inferred and medium confidence.

## Dyeing process: separate universal technical substrate

The existing XII-century Nerev evidence for **coloured wool threads** is not
evidence for a named dye, mordant, dye material, dye-bath, or dyeing operation.
It remains only the historical-colour envelope. The following distinct,
universal process substrate was independently checked in Renée Dancause, Janet
Wagner and Jan Vuori, [*Caring for textiles and costumes*](https://www.canada.ca/en/conservation-institute/services/preventive-conservation/guidelines-collections/textiles-costumes.html),
Canadian Conservation Institute, section **“Dyes, finishes and
embellishments.”** The full page and section were opened through
browser-harness on 2026-09-04.

These are a separate material/process layer. They may compose with the
independently attested historical compatibility of coloured wool only when a
real textile and the required material/process basis are separately supplied;
they do not retroactively identify the Nerev thread's colourant or manufacture.
No colour-specific recipe is proposed.

The three candidates also do not duplicate the approved generic relation
`clay-spindle-whorl → attested_use → spinning`: they supply a separate
stem/fibre-preparation substrate and a historically bounded bridge to spinning.

## Addendum: dated Novgorod dye identifications — candidate research only

This addendum is intentionally narrower than the preceding universal dyeing
relations. It identifies analysed colourants in excavated Novgorod textiles;
it does not make every coloured thread locally dyed, identify a present dye
stock, grant a craft skill, or provide a dye recipe.

### Full scholarly source read

Adam Nahlik, **«Ткани Новгорода. Опыт технологического анализа»**, in
*Труды Новгородской археологической экспедиции*, *Материалы и исследования
по археологии СССР* 123 (1963), printed pp. 228–313. The complete article was
available in a document reproduction at
[StudFiles](https://studfile.net/preview/12819794/); relevant page views were
[printed p. 266](https://studfile.net/preview/12819794/page:10/),
[p. 267](https://studfile.net/preview/12819794/page:11/) and
[p. 268](https://studfile.net/preview/12819794/page:12/). The official IA RAS
scan URL, [MIA_123.pdf](https://archaeolog.ru/media/series/mia/MIA_123.pdf),
was also opened, but its browser viewer remained blank and is not used as an
extraction anchor. The article’s introduction/chronology (p. 228–229) and its
complete contiguous dye-analysis section (pp. 266–268) were read directly;
only those passages support candidates below.

Nahlik sets the Nerev stratigraphic chronology explicitly: levels 20–17 are
XII century; the XII/XIII boundary is level 16; levels 15–12 are XIII century
(printed p. 229). His analysis was undertaken by the Budapest Textile Industry
Research Institute for fourteen textile fragments from the Novgorod excavations
(p. 266). This is direct archaeological analytical evidence, but the study also
flags some textiles as likely imported. Accordingly, a detected dye supports a
dated Novgorod textile context, not local production or local plant supply.

### Exclusions and verification target

- The candidates do not duplicate FPD-04/05’s universal application/mordant
  mechanics or the existing Nerev coloured-wool fact.
- They do not establish a broad Rus recipe, mandatory dye stage, household
  occupation, economic stock, textile provenance, or a particular textile in
  1230.
- FPD-08’s imported-textile warning is material: a verifier should reject any
  normalisation that turns the find into local Novgorod indigo production.
- FPD-06/07 use the author’s early-medieval local-context inference and must
  retain medium confidence rather than be promoted as exact stratified 1230
  availability.

### Composition boundary

This candidate is the missing plain physical transformation between an already
prepared fibre and yarn/thread. It may compose only after state establishes
the fibre and action; dated flax/hemp processing, spindle-whorl evidence, or
coloured textiles do not by themselves establish a current stock, spinner,
tool, completed thread, or historical technique. Independent verification is
required before authoring.
