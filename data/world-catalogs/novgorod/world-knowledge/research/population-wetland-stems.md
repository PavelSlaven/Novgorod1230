# Wetland non-woody vegetation — bounded research

**Status:** candidate research only; no self-approval or production edit.
This is a historical vegetation/source-context pass, not evidence that a
particular scene contains reeds, a harvestable stand, a gathered bundle, or a
historical tying recipe.

## Existing-corpus boundary

The active corpus already has universal *Phragmites* wetland biology,
local-meadow/hay context, gathered-plant context, and ordinary cordage/bast
relations.  Those establish neither a date-compatible Novgorod wet-ground
vegetation occurrence nor an exact reed stand, and are not repeated here.

## Source independently opened

- E. A. Spiridonova and A. S. Aleshinskaya, “Results of Palynological
  Investigations of the Archaeological Sites in the Lake Ilmen and Lake
  Kubenskoye Study Areas,” in M. A. Brisbane, N. A. Makarov and E. N. Nosov
  (eds.), [*The Archaeology of Medieval Novgorod in Context: Studies of
  Centre/Periphery Relations*](https://dokumen.pub/the-archaeology-of-medieval-novgorod-in-context-studies-of-centre-periphery-relations-9781842172780-2011050182.html),
  2012, pp. 10–42; p. 21, “Change in the vegetation in the environs of Ryurik
  Gorodishche around Lake Ilmen.”  Full chapter/source text was opened on
  2026-09-04.  The authors describe four consecutive archaeological pollen
  sections at Ryurik Gorodishche, 2 km south of Novgorod, covering habitation
  levels from the eighth/ninth centuries through the thirteenth century.
  For the beginning of the thirteenth century, they infer considerable
  waterlogging, particularly on lower ground, from the constant presence of
  sphagnum mosses, horsetail, and *Spiraea* (*Filipendula*) among herbaceous
  plants.

## Relation to Monk/Johnston

The existing Monk/Johnston 2012 non-wood-plant source was rechecked as the
requested lead: its already compiled claims concern sampled Troitsky plant
remains and gathered/woodland context, not a dated wet-ground stem source.
WLS-01 therefore uses a separate chapter in the same archaeological volume
with an explicit Lake Ilmen/Ryurik Gorodishche palaeobotanical chronology.

## Composition boundary

WLS-01 can only narrow the historical regional envelope for independently
established wet-ground vegetation.  Universal reed biology may describe an
already established reed; it cannot combine with WLS-01 to materialize reed
at an arbitrary scene.  Any resulting gathered or tied object still requires
real source state, access, material, and ordinary action resolution.
