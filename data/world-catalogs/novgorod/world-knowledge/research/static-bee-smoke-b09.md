# Static bee-smoke B09 — source notes

Candidate author: `/root/bee_smoke_b09_candidate`. Scope is universal modern honey-bee biology during colony handling; it does not attest a 1230 Novgorod tool, hive, colony, fuel, practice, or outcome.

## Sources and bounded findings

- Visscher, Vetter, and Robinson, *Alarm pheromone perception in honey bees is decreased by smoke* (1995), University of Illinois research record: <https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/alarm-pheromone-perception-in-honey-bees-is-decreased-by-smoke-hy/>. Smoke reduced antennal response to alarm pheromones and the response recovered gradually; authors describe this as one component of reduced nest defense. Candidate claim: temporary reduction of alarm-pheromone perception and defensive response, never guaranteed calm.
- Utah State University Extension, [*Thriving Hives: The Apiary — Beekeeping Essentials*](https://extension.usu.edu/beekeeping/research/thriving-hives-the-apiary-beekeeping-essentials). Smoke can prompt bees to gorge on honey, making them less likely to sting, though some may remain active. Candidate claim: possible honey engorgement and reduced stinging likelihood, not a universal response.
- University of Arizona Cooperative Extension, [*Working the Hive*](https://extension.arizona.edu/sites/default/files/2024-08/az2013-2022.pdf), “Smoking”; Tufts University, [*A Way to Bee*](https://vet.tufts.edu/news-events/news/way-bee). Arizona warns that over-smoking can irritate bees; Tufts says smoke too hot for a bare hand is too hot for bees. Candidate claim: excess smoke can irritate and hot smoke is unsuitable, with no threshold or injury assertion.
