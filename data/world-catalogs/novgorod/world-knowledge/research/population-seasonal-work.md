# Seasonal work in the Novgorod/Rus envelope — bounded negative research

**Status:** research only. This pass sought one historical relation connecting
an activity with a season, not an exact date, work roster, required labour, or
present NPC routine. No new promotable candidate was found from the sources
actually read.

## Sources actually read

1. E. A. Rybina, [«Промыслы в средневековом Новгороде (по
   археологическим материалам)»](https://cyberleninka.ru/article/n/promysly-v-srednevekovom-novgorode-po-arheologicheskim-materialam), *Исторические исследования* 3 (2015), printed pp. 219–231. The full public article was re-opened. It gives broad medieval Novgorod fishing/hunting context and dated/period-spanning gear observations, but does **not** give a qualified seasonal work relation for agriculture, haymaking, winter fishing or timber work in the passages read.
2. B. A. Kolchin, *Черная металлургия и металлообработка в Древней Руси
   (домонгольский период)*, MIA 32 (1953), printed pp. 88–98, read from the
   locally available full PDF/OCR corresponding to the [IA RAS
   record](https://archaeolog.ru/el-bib/el-cat/el-series/mia/kolchin-1953).
   It explicitly links sickles and scythes to harvesting crops and grasses
   (p. 90), but does **not** state a season for that work.
3. Two focused browser searches for a primary/academic dated Novgorod/Rus
   seasonal relation—one on sowing/harvest chronology and one on winter timber
   felling/dendrochronology—were performed. Search summaries and AI overviews
   were deliberately rejected: they were not independently opened full
   primary/academic evidence, and no usable dated full-text source was reached
   in this bounded pass.

## Exact remaining gap

The missing premise is not a general fact that plants, ice, birds or daylight
are seasonal; those domains already have separate support. It is a **dated
historical activity-to-season relation** for medieval Novgorod/Rus, supported
by a full source (for example, an excavated/dendrochronologically documented
winter timber-work episode, or a date-qualified textual/archaeological record
of an agricultural/fishing seasonal practice). Such a fact would still only
give a contextual compatibility envelope: actual weather, date, task, worker,
tool, access, stock, output and schedule remain state/mechanics/NPC owners.

## Follow-up: primary chronicle event (1230)

4. Robert Michell and Nevill Forbes (trans.), [*The Chronicle of Novgorod
   1016–1471*](https://archive.org/download/chronicleofnovgo00michrich/chronicleofnovgo00michrich_djvu.txt)
   (London: Camden Society, 1914), p. 74, entry **A.D. 1230**. The complete
   entry was independently read in the digitised volume. It dates the event to
   the Feast of the Exaltation (the translator's note identifies this as
   September 26) and says that “a frost killed the crops throughout our
   district,” followed by misery and grain prices. This is a chronicle report
   of a particular adverse year, not an agricultural calendar or a general
   prediction.

This source closes a narrow **seasonal agricultural vulnerability** premise,
not the broader missing routine relation “harvesting normally occurs in season
X.” SW-01 through SW-03 therefore remain unresolved as written.
