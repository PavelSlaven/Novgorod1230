# Final static R7: reusable safety, material and availability premises

Status: candidate editorial reconstruction. This note responds only to the
fourteen reusable families found by corpus-blind R7 cross-audit. It is not a
recipe, a diagnosis, a present-scene observation, or evidence that any person,
animal, food, fuel, vessel, tool, shelter, route, weather, or authority exists.

## Modern factual bases

The candidate condenses ordinary modern physical chemistry and materials
science (oxidation, gas pressure, copper-acid contact, reflection, combustion,
moisture and phase change), environmental safety (confined air, snow-slope
instability, animal-enclosure air), and conservative first-aid principles
(cold injury and suspected snakebite). Household claims use common food and
fibre processing relations: dry wood burns differently from damp wood,
sunlight and repeated wetting can lighten linen, and warmed beeswax can be
separated from coarse comb debris. The potato boundary uses the broad
historical fact that potato was an American crop absent from thirteenth-century
Novgorod/Rus.

These are deliberately qualitative game premises, not professional safety or
medical instructions. They set no temperatures, doses, time, species, slope
rating, food-safety verdict, treatment, yield, repair method, or outcome.

## Candidate reusable premises

- Stored edible or lamp oil can change through age, air and contamination;
  rancid odour, unusual taste or sediment calls for cautious condition reading,
  not a diagnosis or guaranteed safe use.
- A confined low-ventilation natural space can retain harmful or oxygen-poor
  air; a dimming flame or discomfort is a warning cue rather than a safe test.
- Acidic wet food in unlined copper contact can change the food-contact
  surface; this supports avoiding an assumed safe storage condition.
- Snow slopes can release when their layered snow is unstable; warning signs
  or recent loading support reducing exposure without predicting an avalanche.
- An unusual flame colour or fume can indicate an unidentified material or
  condition; it does not identify the material but supports avoiding inhalation
  and direct exposure.
- A sealed food container can swell when gas forms or warms; swelling does not
  diagnose spoilage but makes pressure and handling uncertainty relevant.
- Polished metal can reflect directional sunlight; visibility depends on light,
  orientation, line of sight and intervening conditions, not merely a shiny
  surface.
- Poorly ventilated animal housing with manure and urine can contain irritating
  air; smell or eye/airway discomfort supports ventilation or withdrawal, not a
  chemical diagnosis.
- A possibly frozen body part should be protected from further cold and rough
  rubbing or uncontrolled heating avoided; condition and available care still
  govern what follows.
- A suspected venomous snakebite warrants limiting exertion and seeking help;
  cutting, sucking or constricting the limb is not a reliable response and the
  species or severity remains unknown.
- Fuelwood combustion depends on moisture, density, piece size and air; dry
  dense and dry light woods can differ in ignition and burn character without a
  fixed ranking or output.
- Repeated wetting/rinsing and daylight can gradually lighten some flax linen;
  soil, dye, fibre condition and weather prevent a guaranteed result.
- Gentle warming of beeswax-containing comb can let liquid wax separate from
  some coarse debris by settling or straining; purity, yield and method remain
  open.
- Potato must not be assumed to be a locally available food in Novgorod/Rus in
  1200–1300; its mention is an anachronism boundary, not a present crop list.
