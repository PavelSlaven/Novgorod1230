## Supported general premises

- Dry natural fibre can be a candidate bedding material only conditionally:
  Rutgers Extension calls straw a traditional bedding source, but says material
  selection includes non-irritability, texture and dust, and that bedding should
  remain dry before use. Straw may itself be musty, mouldy, dusty, or carry
  mites. This supports a *clean, dry, condition-checked* grass/straw/reed-like
  bedding candidate; it does not establish any actual material, suitability for
  human sleeping, cleanliness, safety, quantity, or local availability.
- *Phragmites australis* is a region-compatible reed candidate rather than an
  introduced substitute: Kew lists it as native in Central, North, and Northwest
  European Russia. This supports no present reed stand, harvestable stems,
  season, ownership, access, or use as bedding.
- Plant contact hazards need mechanism- and taxon-aware treatment. The existing
  nettle premise concerns stinging trichomes; independently, Cornell states that
  fresh sap of Ranunculaceae may redden, swell, or blister skin. Kew lists
  *Ranunculus sceleratus* as native in North and Northwest European Russia.
  Thus a wetland/meadow plant can be a region-compatible irritant-sap candidate
  without being nettle or an introduced giant hogweed. It does not identify an
  unknown specimen, establish contact, exposure, injury, severity, allergy,
  ingestion, treatment, or outcome.

## Bounded use

Narration may distinguish (1) mechanical sting from trichomes and (2)
irritating fresh sap, and may condition bedding usefulness on material being
dry and non-irritating. It must retain species/part uncertainty until the
existing multi-character identification boundary is met. Do not infer plant
identity, local presence, a safe bed, a contact event, medical condition, or
medical response from these general premises.
