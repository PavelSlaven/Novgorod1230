# Craft preparation: fibre, hide, pottery and stone — research only

**Status:** candidate research only. No authoring, approval, matrix change,
scene inventory, material stock, quarry/pit, workshop, worker, access or
successful craft result follows.

## Existing-corpus boundary

Compiled 420 already has garment fibres/forms, weaving and cordage; leather
footwear/straps and hide-versus-prepared-leather; universal clay/ceramic
response; and stone as a net weight. It also has no compiled historical
preparation/dyeing/tanning or pottery-firing/source chain. The candidates here
do not duplicate those existing uses or turn material science into medieval
availability.

## Sources independently read

1. T. Brorsson, [*Pottery production in the Novgorod region: local traditions
   and foreign influences*](https://www.jstor.org/stable/j.ctvh1dqcg.31), in
   M. A. Brisbane, N. A. Makarov & E. N. Nosov (eds.), *The Archaeology of
   Medieval Novgorod in Context* (Oxbow, 2012), pp. 425–434. The JSTOR record
   and full chapter abstract were opened. Its stated evidence is pottery from
   Rurik Gorodishche and Novgorod; it says many vessels were hand-made from
   suitable local clays and tempers, and describes a long-lived conservative
   technology. The accessible record does not supply a dated pit, kiln firing
   sequence or recipe.
2. V. A. Bulkin & I. V. Antipov, [«Об особенностях организации строительства
   и строительного производства в Новгородской земле в домонгольское
   время»](https://publ.actual-art.org/aptha/ru/article/download/10572/10572/10572),
   *Актуальные проблемы теории и истории искусства* 7 (2017), pp. 410–418,
   DOI [10.18688/aa177-4-40](https://doi.org/10.18688/aa177-4-40). Full OA PDF
   pp. 410–418 was read; PDF p. 415 was rendered. It is about monumental
   pre-Mongol stone-church construction, not household pottery or an ordinary
   raw-material source.
3. M. M. Savenkova, [«Понёвы из средневекового Новгорода (по материалам
   археологических раскопок)»](https://www.gramota.net/article/hss20151942/fulltext),
   *Исторические, философские, политические и юридические науки…* 11(61),
   2015, pp. 151–154. The HTML record/PDF were previously opened and the full
   PDF text read. p. 152 identifies a XII-century Nerev fragment with coloured
   wool threads; it does not identify a dye, mordant, bath, or dyeing sequence.

## Results by real gap

- **Fibre preparation/dyeing:** CPP-01 narrows presence of coloured wool
  thread, but no opened source gives a safe XI–XIII Novgorod dyestuff,
  mordant or preparation sequence. Keep dyeing partial; next research should
  open a textile-technology or dye-residue study with the actual analytical
  pages, not infer a process from colour.
- **Tanning/hide preparation:** no new candidate is promoted. Existing
  footwear evidence distinguishes tanned hide from raw hide, but this pass
  did not obtain a fully opened XII–XIII Novgorod/Northwest-Rus source that
  states a preparation operation. Do not substitute modern conservation or a
  shoe form for historical tanning. Targeted follow-up: A. V. Kurbatov,
  *Leather-working in North-West Russia*, pp. 391–424, needs full-page access
  and exact dated anchors before use.
- **Pottery/clay/firing:** CPP-02–03 establish bounded local material choice
  for handmade pottery, not a firing chain. CPP-05–06 are already compiled
  specialist-building ceramic context, not new ordinary-pottery support.
  Kiln/firing operation evidence remains needed for ordinary
  ceramic process coverage.
- **Ordinary stone working/procurement:** CPP-04 is already compiled for its
  modal, quarry-based, monumental scope. It cannot close ordinary stone/soil
  sourcing or working.

## Non-inferences

No relation above creates a dye plant, oil/fat/resin, tanning bath, kiln,
clay deposit, quarry, brick, pot, coloured garment, worker knowledge, price,
or access. These are reusable historical compatibility relations, not a craft
recipe whitelist.
