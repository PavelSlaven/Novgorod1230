# Rural settlement context — bounded historical research

**Status:** research candidates only.  This is not an approval, production
record, spatial binding, or authority to materialize a village, a pogost, a
field, people, property, or an institutional duty.

## Independent source read

N. A. Makarov (ed.), [*Археология севернорусской деревни X–XIII веков*,
vol. 3, *Палеоэкологические условия, общество и культура*](https://www.archaeolog.ru/media/series/arch-northrus-village/Arch_northrus_village-3.pdf),
IA RAS / Nauka, 2009.  The full official IA RAS PDF was opened through
browser-harness on 2026-09-04; the relevant foreword was visually read at
printed pp. 5–6 (PDF pp. 6–7) from the local matching copy
`.tmp-pr92-sources/northern-village-3.pdf`.

The foreword identifies the volume's X–XIII-century northern-Rus corpus as
more than sixty excavated rural settlements.  It describes the research scope
as their economy, life support, social organization and cultural traditions,
and names formation of agriculture and composition of agricultural crops among
the economic aspects of rural territories.  Its focused evidence is the Minino
microregion around Lake Kubenskoye and a comparative northern-Rus frame; it is
not a direct archaeological record of a Novgorod-Land village in 1230.

## Deliberate hold: pogost

No `pogost` atom is proposed in this pass.  The source read is about excavated
rural settlements and does not establish a pogost's service, administrative,
ecclesiastical, fiscal, or institutional function.  Modern dictionary meaning
or a later administrative back-projection would not close that gap; a separate
dated primary or scholarly source is required.

## Composition boundary

These candidates, if independently approved, would be broad historical
compatibility only.  They could help distinguish a confirmed rural context from
an urban/market/landing context, but cannot substitute for committed spatial
state or any code-owned owner of place, access, resources, people, property,
or institutional authority.
