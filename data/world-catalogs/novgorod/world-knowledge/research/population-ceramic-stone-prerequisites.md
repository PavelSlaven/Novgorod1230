# Ceramic firing and stone-working prerequisites — research only

**Status:** candidate research, not approved or authored.  This pass is
limited to the remaining `wk:process:ceramic-stone` causal substrate.  It
creates no pot, kiln, clay bed, stone stock, quarry, tool, craft worker,
access right, recipe, temperature, duration, or successful result.

## Existing-corpus boundary

I read the active `woodwork-ceramics.json`, `material-response.json`,
`approved-construction.json`, `population-craft-preparation.md`, and the
material/process readiness audits.  The following already have independently
grounded production relations and are **not proposed again**: clay plasticity;
shrinkage and the benefit (not guarantee) of slow even drying; steam rupture
from rapid heating of wet clay; quartz-related cracking; ceramic brittleness,
porosity and water response; body/firing effects on porosity; local-clay and
temper selection for a medieval Novgorod/Rurik-Gorodishche pottery corpus;
and specialist pre-Mongol building ceramics.  In particular, a universal
drying/thermal failure claim cannot by itself establish a medieval kiln or a
scene source of clay.

## Sources independently opened

1. A. V. Artsikhovskii, [*Osnovy arkheologii*, chapter 12, “Rus' IX–XIII vv.”
   (1955)](http://historic.ru/books/item/f00/s00/z0000204/st013.shtml).
   The accessible full chapter was read.  In its paragraph comparing the
   material culture of Rus' and Scandinavia, it explicitly says that pottery
   in Kyiv, Smolensk and **Novgorod** in the ninth–tenth centuries was already
   made on the potter's wheel.  This is a dated historical synthesis, earlier
   than 1230; it does not describe firing, a workshop, or an individual vessel.
2. Elisabeth H. West, [“Jade: Its Character and Occurrence”](https://www.penn.museum/sites/expedition/jade/),
   *Expedition* 5(2), University of Pennsylvania Museum, originally published
   1963.  The full museum article was read, especially the section beginning
   “The raw material was first broken and trimmed...” and the following
   discussion of archaeological reconstruction in British Columbia/Alaska.
   It reports hard-stone working methods reconstructed from tools and partly
   worked material.  It is evidence for conditional physical technology, **not
   for medieval Rus' availability**.

## Composition and limits

`CSP-01` is the **historical** layer: a potter’s wheel is a documented earlier
Novgorod technology, so its later compatibility can be considered only with
the stated medium inference.  `CSP-02`–`CSP-04` are **universal
physical-technology** premises: they never prove that medieval Novgorod had
the relevant hard stone, abrasive, tool, workshop, or a current object to
alter.  They also do not substitute for existing clay drying/firing facts.

The actual remaining historical gap is consequently narrow: this pass did
not locate an independently opened, dated Novgorod/Rus source that documents
an ordinary-pot firing installation or firing operation.  That absence does
not make an ordinary household structure impossible; it merely means no such
fact should be promoted from this packet.

## Suggested verifier focus

- Keep existing clay drying, steam, quartz and ceramic-response claims as
