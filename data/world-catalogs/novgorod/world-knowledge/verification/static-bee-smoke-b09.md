## Approved premises

- `claim:static-bee-smoke-b09-smoke-can-temporarily-reduce-alarm-pheromone-perception-and-defense` — **APPROVE.** Visscher, Vetter and Robinson directly report reversible reduction of *Apis mellifera* antennal response after smoke and identify it as one component of reduced nest defense. It does not promise calm, access, a dose, a duration for a scene, or a historical practice. [Illinois record](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/alarm-pheromone-perception-in-honey-bees-is-decreased-by-smoke-hy/)
- `claim:static-bee-smoke-b09-smoke-can-prompt-honey-engorgement-without-guaranteeing-calm` — **APPROVE.** USU describes honey engorgement and lower stinging likelihood after smoke, while allowing active bees to remain. This is a conditional modern handling relation, not a safety guarantee or a claim about a particular colony. [USU Extension](https://extension.usu.edu/beekeeping/research/thriving-hives-the-apiary-beekeeping-essentials)
- `claim:static-bee-smoke-b09-excessive-smoke-can-irritate-and-hot-smoke-is-unsuitable` — **APPROVE.** Arizona separately supports irritation from over-smoking; Tufts separately gives the bare-hand heat limit. Neither source supplies a temperature, dose, injury conclusion, procedure, or medieval device. [Arizona Extension](https://extension.arizona.edu/sites/default/files/2024-08/az2013-2022.pdf), [Tufts](https://vet.tufts.edu/news-events/news/way-bee)

All three remain modern *Apis mellifera* factual context. They do not establish a 1230 Novgorod hive, smoker, fuel, colony, access, stock, or scene outcome.
