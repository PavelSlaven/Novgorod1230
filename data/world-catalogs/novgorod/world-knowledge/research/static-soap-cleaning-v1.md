# Soap cleaning: bounded general premises

Status: research note only. No historical availability, recipe, quantity, heating protocol, textile outcome or certain cleaning result follows.

## Alkaline hydrolysis of fats and oils

Source: University of Washington Department of Chemistry, [“Saponification”](https://chem.washington.edu/lecture-demos/saponification), **Discussion**, lines 27–29.

The page defines saponification as alkaline hydrolysis of glyceryl trialkanoates, forming glycerol and salts of long-chain carboxylic acids; those salts are soaps. It demonstrates the chemistry, not wood-ash strength, a usable lye, a historical process, a quantity, a heating protocol or a cleaning outcome.

## Soap structure and oil dispersion

Source: Royal Society of Chemistry Education, [“How does washing detergent work?”](https://edu.rsc.org/everyday-chemistry/how-does-washing-detergent-work/4018846.article), **How do detergents work?** and **Washing clothes**.

Hydrophilic heads and hydrophobic tails can surround an oil/grease droplet into a micelle, allowing it to be carried away in water. This supports only a general removal mechanism; it does not prove that a given stain, wool textile, water, agitation or rinse will succeed.
